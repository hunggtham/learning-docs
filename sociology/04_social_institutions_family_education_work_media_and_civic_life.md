# Social Institutions — Family, Education, Work, Media và Civic Life

Institutions organize recurring social problems: care, reproduction, learning, production, communication, collective decision and belonging. Sociology studies how these systems distribute roles/resources, how formal rules interact with informal norms, and how institutions reinforce or transform one another.

This chapter keeps institutional mechanisms general and cross-links domain-specific legal/economic/history material instead of duplicating it.

## 1. Family as institution

Family organizes care, reproduction, socialization, emotional support, inheritance and resource pooling.

Family forms vary historically and cross-culturally; one household form should not be treated as universal baseline.

## 2. Household vs family

Household is co-residential/economic unit; family is kinship/recognized relational structure.

They overlap but are not identical.

## 3. Division of household labor

Paid work and unpaid care/housework are jointly allocated within households.

Outcomes depend wages, bargaining power, norms, policy and life-course events.

## 4. Bargaining inside households

Household decisions need not reflect one shared utility. Resources/outside options can influence bargaining.

Who receives income or legal rights can affect spending and autonomy.

## 5. Marriage and partnership markets

Partnering patterns reflect preferences, social networks, institutions and opportunity structures.

Educational assortative mating can increase household-level inequality when high earners pair with high earners.

## 6. Fertility

Fertility decisions interact with education, housing, childcare, labor markets, gender norms and uncertainty.

Fertility rate changes should not be explained by one cultural attitude without institutional mechanisms.

## 7. Intergenerational transmission

Families transmit wealth, language, health, expectations, networks and cultural capital.

Transmission can occur before formal schooling begins.

## 8. Education as institution

Education transmits knowledge/skills, credentials, norms and social networks.

It can promote mobility while also reproducing inequalities in preparation, tracking and school quality.

## 9. School sorting

Residential location, admissions, tracking and private resources sort students across peer/school environments.

Observed school differences combine composition and institution effects.

## 10. Tracking

Grouping students by ability/performance can target instruction but may lock in early measurement errors and unequal expectations.

Effects depend mobility between tracks and resource quality.

## 11. Credentialing

Degrees signal standardized achievement and facilitate selection.

Credential inflation occurs when higher credentials become required for jobs without equivalent skill change.

## 12. Peer effects

Peers can affect norms, information, competition and learning.

But students select schools/friends, so peer similarity is not automatically influence.

## 13. Work as institution

Work organizes income, time, status, identity and social networks.

Employment relation also allocates authority and risk.

## 14. Occupational communities

Professions/occupations develop norms, language and identities beyond individual firms.

Professional norms can constrain employer authority.

## 15. Emotional labor

Jobs may require workers to manage/display emotions according to organizational expectations.

This creates effort often invisible in task descriptions.

## 16. Alienation

Alienation frameworks examine loss of control/meaning in work when tasks/products/processes are separated from worker autonomy.

Modern applications should specify autonomy, task fragmentation or value conflict rather than use term generically.

## 17. Labor process

Organizations design workflows to coordinate/control production.

Technology can increase autonomy for some workers and surveillance/standardization for others.

## 18. Workplace inequality

Hiring, promotion, informal networks, scheduling and performance evaluation can create cumulative differences.

Organizational routines matter beyond individual prejudice.

## 19. Precarity and risk shifting

Temporary/gig/on-call arrangements can shift demand/income risk from firms to workers.

Social protection and bargaining institutions alter consequences.

## 20. Religion as institution

Religion can organize meaning, community, moral norms, charity and authority.

Secularization is multidimensional: belief, practice, institutional authority and public influence may change differently.

## 21. Religious markets/networks

Participation can be shaped by social networks, migration and organizational competition.

Avoid reducing religion to private belief only.

## 22. Media as institution

Media organizations select, frame and distribute information under professional, commercial, political and technological constraints.

Media effects depend audience selection and network diffusion.

## 23. Agenda setting

Media can influence what issues people consider salient without fully determining opinions.

Salience and persuasion are different mechanisms.

## 24. Framing

How an issue is categorized/presented affects interpretation by highlighting certain causes, values or solutions.

Frame effects depend prior beliefs and context.

## 25. Algorithms and platforms

Recommendation systems shape exposure while users self-select content.

Observed polarization/filtering can reflect both algorithmic ranking and user preferences/network homophily.

## 26. Civic associations

Clubs, unions, professional groups, neighborhood organizations and NGOs create social ties and collective capacity.

Associational life can build trust/skills but also mobilize exclusionary goals.

## 27. Civil society

Civil society refers organizations/relations between households, markets and state that enable collective action and public expression.

Its boundary varies across institutional contexts.

## 28. Political socialization

Family, school, peers, media and institutions shape civic knowledge and participation norms.

This is descriptive sociology; it should not be used to steer political choice.

## 29. Participation

Civic participation depends resources, recruitment networks, institutional access and perceived efficacy.

Participation inequalities can amplify resource inequalities.

## 30. Organizations and representation

Formal representation structures can differ from whose preferences are actually heard.

Analyze selection, agenda-setting and organizational capacity rather than assume equal voice.

## 31. Law as social institution

Law formalizes categories, rights and sanctions but implementation depends organizations, enforcement and legal consciousness.

Detailed legal content belongs in law domains; sociology studies social operation of law.

## 32. Medical/health institutions

Healthcare organizes expertise, professional authority, access and classification of illness.

Health outcomes also depend social determinants outside medical care.

## 33. Institutional interdependence

Family policy affects labor supply; education affects work/status; housing shapes schools; media shapes public attention; welfare policy changes household risks.

Institutional analysis should trace cross-system effects.

## 34. Institutional change

Change can come from demographic shifts, social movements, technology, legal reform, economic shocks and organizational diffusion.

Formal reform may be decoupled from practice if implementation incentives unchanged.

## 35. Institutional trust

Trust can reflect performance, fairness, identity and repeated experience.

Low trust may reduce compliance/cooperation; forced compliance can also affect trust.

## 36. Comparative institutions

Same nominal institution—school, family policy, union, media system—can function differently under different financing/governance regimes.

Cross-country labels need operational detail.

## 37. Institutional data

Evidence can combine administrative records, surveys, organizations, interviews, policy documents and longitudinal outcomes.

Formal policy text alone is insufficient for implementation claims.

## 38. Failure modes

Sai lầm thứ nhất là treat family/school/work as isolated systems.

Sai lầm thứ hai là infer institution effect from composition differences alone.

Sai lầm thứ ba là equate formal policy with actual practice.

Sai lầm thứ tư là explain media influence without separating selection from exposure.

Sai lầm thứ năm là treat civic participation as equally accessible across groups.

## 39. Analysis template

1. institution solves/organizes recurring problem nào?
2. roles/rules/resources được phân bố ra sao?
3. formal vs informal practices khác nhau ở đâu?
4. selection/composition vs institutional effect thế nào?
5. which groups gain/lose access/authority?
6. cross-institution spillovers nào?
7. organization implements rule bằng incentives nào?
8. technology/demography change institution ra sao?
9. comparative regime differences nào?
10. evidence observes policy, practice hay outcome?

Institutions operate within changing populations, cities and global connections. The final sociology core chapter studies demographic change, urbanization, collective behavior and social transformation.
