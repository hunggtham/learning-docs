# Động lực và cảm xúc — Motivation & Emotion / 동기와 정서

Động lực (motivation) và cảm xúc (emotion) không phải hai hệ tách biệt. Động lực giúp hệ thống phân bổ effort và chọn goal; cảm xúc giúp đánh dấu significance, threat, opportunity và progress relative to goal. Cả hai chịu ảnh hưởng của body state, learning history, cognition, social context và culture.

> **Trạng thái bằng chứng tổng quát:** reward learning, expectancy/value, approach–avoidance, appraisal và emotion regulation đều có substantial empirical support. Tuy nhiên emotion science hiện không có một grand theory duy nhất được consensus. Basic-emotion, appraisal, constructionist và predictive approaches giải thích overlapping nhưng không identical phenomena.

## 1. “Thiếu động lực” không phải mechanism

Khi một người nói “tôi không có động lực”, ít nhất các possibilities sau có thể khác nhau:

- goal value thấp;
- expectancy of success thấp;
- goal mơ hồ;
- competing reward mạnh hơn;
- immediate cost quá cao;
- fatigue/sleep debt;
- anxiety/avoidance;
- lack of autonomy;
- environment có friction lớn;
- clinical depression/anhedonia.

Vì vậy “cố gắng hơn” rarely is a complete explanation.

## 2. Expectancy–value

Các **mô hình kỳ vọng–giá trị (expectancy–value models)** propose action investment depends partly on perceived value and expectation of success.

Một mental model đơn giản:

```text
Motivational pull ≈ value × expectancy − perceived cost
```

Đây không phải physical equation. Nó chỉ giúp phân biệt why high-value goal can still be avoided when confidence is near zero or cost immediate.

> **Current theory family:** expectancy–value models have extensive educational/motivational evidence, but exact components and equations vary by model.

## 3. Self-efficacy

**Tự hiệu quả (self-efficacy)** là belief về khả năng thực hiện hành động cụ thể để đạt outcome, not global self-worth.

Self-efficacy can influence persistence, challenge selection and effort. It is built partly from mastery experience, modeling, feedback and interpretation of physiological state.

High self-efficacy can be useful when calibrated; overconfidence can produce poor planning.

## 4. Intrinsic và extrinsic motivation

**Động lực nội tại (intrinsic motivation)** means activity itself is rewarding/interesting. **Động lực ngoại tại (extrinsic motivation)** means action serves another outcome such as money, grade, certificate or avoidance of punishment.

These are not opposites that cannot coexist.

A developer can enjoy solving technical problems and value salary simultaneously.

## 5. Self-Determination Theory

**Lý thuyết tự quyết (Self-Determination Theory — SDT)** emphasizes quality of motivation and needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness.

- **Autonomy**: action feels volitional/endorsed.
- **Competence**: feeling able to make progress.
- **Relatedness**: connection and belonging.

> **Current theory with broad research program:** SDT has substantial supportive evidence across domains, but needs should not be treated as simple universal switches explaining every outcome.

Autonomy does not mean “no rules”. Clear constraints can coexist with meaningful choice and rationale.

## 6. Goal conflict

Apparent laziness can be competition between legitimate goals: learning, recovery, family, money, leisure.

A useful analysis:

```text
Which goal has immediate reward?
Which goal has immediate cost?
Which cue is active?
What is energy state?
What opportunity cost is perceived?
```

Self-regulation often means restructuring conflict rather than increasing willpower.

## 7. Temporal discounting

Humans often value immediate outcomes more than delayed outcomes. **Temporal discounting** helps explain procrastination, saving difficulty and health behavior.

Discounting is not always irrational: future rewards are uncertain. Bias arises when short-term weighting conflicts with person's own stable long-term goals.

## 8. Reward learning and dopamine

Dopamine is not simply “pleasure chemical”. Dopaminergic systems participate in movement, motivation, salience and reward learning.

A key computational concept is **reward prediction error**: difference between expected and received outcome helps update value.

> **Established/current distinction:** dopamine-related prediction-error signals are well-supported in learning neuroscience; mapping every human motivation problem to “dopamine level” is pseudoscientific simplification.

## 9. Incentives can change behavior without changing values

External reward can increase target behavior while present. Whether behavior persists after reward removal depends on learning, identity, skill and context.

Claims that “rewards always destroy intrinsic motivation” are too broad. Effects depend task, reward contingency, control perception and baseline interest.

## 10. Approach and avoidance

Motivation includes **approach** toward desired outcomes and **avoidance** of threat/cost.

Avoidance can be adaptive in danger but become self-reinforcing in anxiety because immediate relief prevents learning that feared outcome may be tolerable or unlikely.

## 11. Emotion is multi-component

An emotion episode can involve:

- appraisal;
- bodily change;
- subjective feeling;
- action tendency;
- attention shift;
- expression;
- memory modulation.

No single component alone defines all emotion.

## 12. Basic-emotion approaches

**Basic Emotion Theories (BETs)** propose some emotion families have evolved biological organization and partially distinct expressive/action patterns.

Evidence supports cross-cultural regularities and biologically relevant patterns, but expression is more context-sensitive and multidimensional than popular “six universal faces” diagrams suggest.

> **Current theory:** basic-emotion approaches remain active scientific frameworks, not settled final model.

## 13. Appraisal theories

**Appraisal theories** propose emotional differentiation depends on evaluation of events relative to goals, control, responsibility, novelty and coping potential.

Recent meta-analytic work across hundreds of studies supports many systematic appraisal–emotion associations, while details differ across appraisal models.

> **Substantial evidence for appraisal–emotion relations; exact causal architecture remains theory-dependent.**

## 14. Constructionist approaches

**Constructionist theories** emphasize core affect/body state, conceptual knowledge, context and language in constructing emotion categories.

They challenge idea that each emotion has one fixed physiological/facial signature.

> **Current theory / active debate:** constructionist approaches explain important variability, but should not be presented as having “disproved” all basic-emotion evidence.

## 15. Theories may target different levels

Part of emotion-theory conflict comes from asking different questions:

```text
Evolutionary function?
Immediate elicitation?
Subjective feeling?
Concept/category formation?
Expression?
Neural implementation?
```

A theory can be strong at one level and incomplete at another.

This is why library does not choose a single winner by slogan.

## 16. James–Lange, Cannon–Bard và Schachter–Singer

These are historically important theories illustrating body–brain–interpretation relationships.

Modern affective science is more complex than textbook sequence where one old theory “replaced” another.

Use them to understand history of questions, not as the complete current taxonomy.

## 17. Interoception and emotion

Perception of internal body signals contributes to affective experience.

Heart rate, breathing, gut sensation and arousal are interpreted in context.

> **Current evidence:** interoception matters for emotion and anxiety-related processes, but no single “body signal → exact emotion” mapping exists.

## 18. Action tendencies

Emotion can bias action readiness:

- fear → protection/avoidance;
- anger → approach/confrontation;
- guilt → repair;
- shame → withdrawal/concealment;
- interest → exploration.

These are tendencies, not commands. Context and regulation can override them.

## 19. Emotion and rationality

Emotion is not opposite of reason. Decision requires valuation: what matters, what is risky, what should be prioritized.

Problem occurs when emotional signal reflects outdated learning, misleading cue or extreme state that dominates broader evidence.

## 20. Emotion regulation process model

Gross-style process models distinguish intervention points:

1. situation selection;
2. situation modification;
3. attention deployment;
4. cognitive change/reappraisal;
5. response modulation.

> **Current theory with substantial empirical use:** useful framework, but strategies are not universally good/bad.

## 21. Reappraisal

**Tái đánh giá nhận thức (cognitive reappraisal)** changes interpretation of event.

On average it often relates to more adaptive emotional outcome than chronic expressive suppression, but context matters.

Reappraising real danger as “nothing” can be maladaptive. Effective reappraisal should update meaning using evidence, not forced positivity.

## 22. Expressive suppression

**Ức chế biểu hiện (expressive suppression)** reduces visible expression after emotion arises.

It can have social/cognitive costs in some contexts, but may be strategically useful in situations requiring temporary composure or cultural display rules.

No strategy should be moralized independent of context.

## 23. Regulatory flexibility

**Linh hoạt điều chỉnh cảm xúc (regulatory flexibility)** means selecting strategy based on controllability, social context, intensity and goal.

> **Current theory/construct:** flexibility is increasingly emphasized because fixed strategy rankings fail across contexts. Measurement and causal mechanisms remain active research.

## 24. Acceptance and avoidance

Trying to eliminate internal experiences can paradoxically increase monitoring and struggle.

Acceptance-based approaches distinguish feeling from action: emotion can be present without behavior being dictated by it.

This supports everyday self-regulation but should not be turned into “accept everything” slogan.

## 25. Emotion granularity

**Độ phân biệt cảm xúc (emotion granularity)** is ability to distinguish emotional states more specifically.

Higher granularity is associated in some literature with better regulation/wellbeing, but causality and measurement vary.

> **Evidence status:** promising association/current research, not universal causal law.

## 26. Affective forecasting

People often mispredict intensity/duration of future emotion because they focus on focal event and underweight adaptation/other life events.

This affects career, relationship and purchase decisions.

But forecasting error magnitude varies; humans are not uniformly poor predictors.

## 27. Stress and emotion

Stress changes attention, arousal and control. Acute stress can facilitate performance on some simple/well-learned tasks but impair flexible control under other conditions.

“Stress is good” and “stress is bad” are both too broad.

## 28. Motivation and depression

Depression can involve reduced reward sensitivity, anhedonia, fatigue, hopelessness and avoidance, so “motivation problem” may reflect clinical syndrome.

Self-discipline advice should not replace assessment when impairment is broad/severe.

## 29. Social motivation

Belonging, status, approval and fairness strongly influence action. Human motivation is not only individual reward maximization.

Team behavior may depend on psychological safety and social identity more than financial incentive alone.

Xem [[10_group_dynamics_collective_behavior_and_cooperation]] and [[../06_applied/16_psychological_safety_team_learning_and_speaking_up]].

## 30. Culture

Culture shapes which goals are normative, how emotion is labeled/displayed and what autonomy/relatedness mean in practice.

Cross-cultural variation does not mean biology absent; it means expression and interpretation occur in social systems.

## 31. Adler connection: similarity without validation

Adler emphasized goal direction, inferiority/compensation and social interest.

Modern motivation research studies goals, self-efficacy, status and belonging, but this does not validate Adler's entire Individual Psychology.

## 32. Everyday application: mechanism first

If user wants to study after work, ask:

```text
Low value?
Low expectancy?
High immediate effort cost?
Fatigue?
Competing reward?
Unclear next action?
Anxiety/avoidance?
```

Different mechanism → different intervention.

This is more evidence-aligned than “increase motivation”.

## 33. Common misconceptions

### “Dopamine is happiness chemical”

Sai simplification.

### “Intrinsic motivation is always better”

No. External goals can be adaptive and internalized.

### “One emotion theory is scientifically proven”

No. Emotion science has active competing/integrative frameworks.

### “Reappraisal means positive thinking”

No. It means changing interpretation, ideally consistent with evidence and goal.

### “Emotion should be controlled away”

No. Emotion supplies information/action tendency; regulation concerns relationship between emotion, context and action.
