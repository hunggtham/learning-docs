# Phát triển suốt vòng đời — Lifespan Development / 발달심리학

Tâm lý học phát triển (developmental psychology) nghiên cứu **sự thay đổi và tính ổn định của con người từ trước khi sinh đến tuổi già**. Phát triển không phải một chiếc thang mà mọi người leo theo cùng thứ tự và tốc độ. Nó là quá trình động, trong đó sinh học, học tập, quan hệ, văn hóa, điều kiện kinh tế–xã hội và lịch sử cá nhân liên tục tác động qua lại.

> **Trạng thái bằng chứng tổng quát:** sự phát triển phụ thuộc đồng thời vào maturation, experience và context là bằng chứng tương đối vững. Các mô hình stage cứng, “một độ tuổi quyết định tất cả” hoặc claim về một sensitive period duy nhất cho mọi chức năng đều quá đơn giản. Sensitive periods có evidence mạnh ở một số systems, nhưng timing và reversibility khác nhau theo domain.

## 1. Phát triển là quá trình, không phải danh sách mốc

Developmental science quan tâm ít nhất bốn câu hỏi:

1. **Điều gì thay đổi?** cognition, emotion, body, identity, relationship, role.
2. **Điều gì tương đối ổn định?** temperament, trait rank-order, learned skill, social pattern.
3. **Cơ chế nào tạo change/stability?** maturation, learning, selection, feedback, socialization.
4. **Thời gian và context nào làm effect khác đi?** sensitive period, transition, cohort, culture.

Một milestone có thể hữu ích như statistical reference, nhưng không phải deadline tuyệt đối cho every individual.

## 2. Hệ động và mô hình giao dịch

Một cách hiểu hiện đại là coi person và environment như một **hệ động (dynamic system)**:

```text
Sinh học + trạng thái trước đó + môi trường + quan hệ
                         ↓
                 thích nghi hiện tại
                         ↓
         behavior làm environment thay đổi
                         ↓
                 đầu vào mới
```

**Mô hình giao dịch (transactional model)** nhấn mạnh child không chỉ nhận environment. Temperament của trẻ có thể evoke caregiver response; caregiver response lại ảnh hưởng self-regulation của trẻ; loop tiếp tục qua thời gian.

Điều này tránh hai cực đoan:

- “parents determine everything”;
- “genes determine everything”.

## 3. Timescale là một phần của theory

Development xảy ra trên nhiều thang thời gian:

- milliseconds/seconds: attention, physiological response;
- hours/days: learning, sleep, stress recovery;
- months: skill acquisition, relationship change;
- years: puberty, identity, education, career;
- decades: aging, cumulative exposure.

Một design đo mỗi 5 năm không thể capture same process as experience-sampling mỗi ngày. Vì vậy **choice of timescale is theoretical**, không chỉ technical.

> **Current methodological principle:** theory, sampling frequency và statistical model phải match timescale của process được hypothesize.

## 4. Nature–nurture là false dichotomy

Genes và environment không hoạt động độc lập.

**Gene–environment interaction (G×E)**: effect of environment differs by genotype, or genetic effect depends environment.

**Gene–environment correlation (rGE)**: dispositions and family inheritance shape which environments people encounter.

**Developmental plasticity**: same genotype can produce different phenotype under different developmental conditions.

Do đó câu “trait này bao nhiêu % do gene?” thường bị hiểu sai nếu chuyển population variance statistic thành causal share của một individual.

## 5. Sensitive period và critical period

**Giai đoạn nhạy cảm (sensitive period)** là khoảng thời gian experience có ảnh hưởng tương đối mạnh hoặc learning dễ hơn.

**Giai đoạn tới hạn (critical period)** mạnh hơn: nếu input không xuất hiện trong window hẹp, later development có thể severely constrained.

Human development có evidence rõ về timing effects trong sensory systems và một số language functions, nhưng “critical period” không nên dùng như generic slogan cho mọi skill.

> **Established evidence:** plasticity varies across development.
>
> **Debated/conditional:** exact windows, mechanisms và reversibility differ by system and exposure.

## 6. Phát triển trước sinh

Prenatal development chịu influence của nutrition, infection, toxins, medication, endocrine state, maternal health và placental function.

**Tác nhân gây dị tật (teratogen)** có effect phụ thuộc:

- dose;
- timing;
- duration;
- genetic susceptibility;
- interacting exposures.

Exposure is probabilistic risk, not deterministic destiny.

## 7. Birth outcomes và developmental inference

Preterm birth, low birth weight or prenatal complication can predict later risk on average, but prediction individual has uncertainty.

Many children show resilience; postnatal healthcare, family resources and intervention modify trajectory.

A risk marker is not a fixed developmental fate.

## 8. Infancy: perception, action và social learning

Infants learn through coordinated perception and action. Reaching, crawling and walking change what can be explored; motor development therefore changes cognitive/social input.

This illustrates **developmental cascade**: progress in one domain alters opportunities in another.

## 9. Early social interaction

Face-to-face interaction, contingent response and caregiver regulation contribute to early communication and emotional development.

But claim “one exact parenting style permanently wires the brain” usually exceeds evidence. Child temperament, caregiver stress, culture and broader environment matter.

Xem [[01_attachment_and_relationships]] và [[14_parenting_caregiving_and_family_development]].

## 10. Attachment

Attachment theory describes how children organize proximity-seeking and safe-base behavior toward caregivers.

> **Established evidence:** attachment relationships show patterned differences and caregiving sensitivity matters probabilistically.
>
> **Limitation:** infant attachment category is not permanent personality type and should not be used as internet diagnosis of adult relationships.

## 11. Language development

Children learn language from biological capacity interacting with rich social/statistical input.

Word segmentation, phonological categories, grammar and pragmatics develop on different trajectories.

Early exposure matters, particularly phonological tuning, but adults remain capable of second-language learning. Accent-like proficiency may become harder without making later learning impossible.

## 12. Piaget: historical importance and modern revision

Piaget proposed stages of cognitive development and emphasized active construction of knowledge.

> **Historical/current distinction:** rigid stage timing and some task interpretations have been revised. Modern developmental science supports active learning and qualitative representational change, but not a simple universal staircase exactly matching classical stages.

Task demand, language and executive function can make young children appear less competent than they are.

## 13. Vygotsky and sociocultural development

Vygotsky emphasized language, tools and social mediation.

**Vùng phát triển gần (Zone of Proximal Development — ZPD)** distinguishes what learner can do alone from what becomes possible with support.

**Chống đỡ học tập (scaffolding)** is later terminology describing adaptive support that fades as competence increases.

This remains influential educational framework, but should not be treated as one precisely quantified biological law.

## 14. Executive functions in childhood

Inhibitory control, working memory and cognitive flexibility improve substantially across childhood.

These skills are influenced by maturation, schooling, sleep, stress, nutrition and practice.

Training task performance does not automatically produce large far-transfer gains to general intelligence or life outcome.

## 15. Adolescence: more than “immature brain”

Adolescence involves puberty, social reorientation, identity exploration, autonomy negotiation and changing reward sensitivity.

Popular claim “brain is not developed until 25” is oversimplified. Different networks and functions mature at different rates; development continues beyond one birthday.

Adolescents can reason well in calm settings but peer/emotional context may change risk weighting.

## 16. Peer influence

Peer presence can increase risk-taking in some contexts but can also support prosocial behavior, learning and belonging.

> **Evidence status:** peer influence is real but direction depends norm and group. “Peers make adolescents reckless” is too broad.

## 17. Identity development

Adolescence and emerging adulthood often involve exploration of values, occupation, relationship, culture and group belonging.

Identity is not completed once and for all at age 18 or 25. Migration, career transition, parenthood and loss can trigger later reorganization.

Xem [[09_self_concept_identity_and_self_regulation]] và [[16_acculturation_migration_and_bicultural_identity]].

## 18. Emerging adulthood

The concept **emerging adulthood** describes a prolonged transition in some societies between adolescence and stable adult roles.

> **Current/debated framework:** it describes many industrialized contexts well but is not universal developmental stage. Economic structure, education, culture and family expectations shape whether this period exists.

## 19. Adult development continues

Adult life includes changes in expertise, role, partnership, caregiving, health, work and meaning.

Development can be:

- gain in one domain;
- loss in another;
- reorganization rather than linear improvement.

Career expertise can grow while processing speed changes. Social network can become smaller while relationship selectivity increases.

## 20. Midlife

“Midlife crisis” is not a universal stage.

Some people experience role overload, caregiving, career reevaluation or health change; others show high stability. Life events and socioeconomic context often explain more than chronological age alone.

## 21. Aging is multidimensional

Aging involves biological, cognitive and social changes with substantial heterogeneity.

Processing speed and episodic memory often decline on average, while semantic knowledge and expertise may remain strong longer.

Individual trajectories differ widely.

Xem [[11_aging_cognitive_health_and_late_life]].

## 22. Cognitive reserve

**Dự trữ nhận thức (cognitive reserve)** is a hypothesis/framework explaining why same brain pathology may relate to different functional outcomes.

Education, occupational complexity, physical activity and social engagement often correlate with reserve-related outcomes.

> **Current theory / observational evidence:** association is substantial, but causal pathway and intervention effect are harder to establish because socioeconomic and health confounding matter.

Do not write “learning prevents dementia”.

## 23. Cohort effects

Cross-sectional age difference can reflect **cohort effect**: education, nutrition, technology, war, labor market or cultural norm differed across generations.

Therefore:

```text
20-year-old vs 70-year-old difference
        ≠ automatically aging effect
```

Longitudinal and cohort-sequential designs help disentangle age, period and cohort, but no design solves all confounding.

## 24. Longitudinal design

Following same people reveals within-person change but suffers attrition, retest effects and historical period confounding.

People who remain in decades-long study may be healthier or more advantaged than dropouts.

## 25. Cross-sectional design

Cross-sectional studies are efficient and useful for description but cannot directly show individual developmental trajectory.

Age-group mean difference should not be narrated as “people change this way” without longitudinal support.

## 26. Developmental cascade

A small early difference can amplify:

```text
Reading difficulty
→ less reading
→ slower vocabulary growth
→ school frustration
→ even less reading
```

Positive cascades also occur:

```text
Support
→ small competence gain
→ more practice
→ stronger self-efficacy
→ more practice
```

Cascades explain why early intervention can matter without implying early life irreversibly determines outcome.

## 27. Cumulative risk

Multiple modest risks can accumulate: poverty, sleep disruption, family conflict, school instability, discrimination.

Risk count can predict outcome, but simple counts hide severity/timing and protective factors.

A developmental formulation should ask which mechanism each exposure affects.

## 28. Resilience

**Khả năng phục hồi (resilience)** is not invulnerability trait. Modern view often treats resilience as positive adaptation despite adversity emerging from person–context systems.

Social support, resources, meaning, skills and opportunity all matter.

Calling someone “not resilient enough” can individualize structural problems.

## 29. Early adversity

Early adversity is associated with later mental and physical health risk, and developmental timing may matter.

> **Established evidence:** early adversity predicts increased average risk.
>
> **Current/debated mechanism:** specific neural/endocrine/immune pathways and exact sensitive windows vary by exposure and outcome.

Adversity ≠ destiny.

## 30. Socioeconomic context

Income, housing, education, pollution, food security, healthcare and neighborhood safety shape developmental opportunity.

These are not mere “background variables”. They influence stress, sleep, learning resources and health exposure.

## 31. Culture

Developmental milestones and goals are partly culturally organized. Independence, obedience, sibling caregiving, co-sleeping or family obligation can have different normative meanings across societies.

Cross-cultural variation does not mean biology irrelevant; it reveals which pattern is context-dependent.

## 32. Migration

Migration can change language, status, family role, network and identity simultaneously. Developmental analysis should include **role transition**, not only age.

Xem [[16_acculturation_migration_and_bicultural_identity]].

## 33. Plasticity across life

Plasticity persists throughout life but is not unlimited. Learning rate, physical constraints, prior knowledge and neural state change with age.

> **Established evidence:** adult learning and neural plasticity exist.
>
> **Limitation:** “you can rewire anything at any age” is an overclaim.

## 34. Developmental timing hypotheses

When an exposure occurs repeatedly, different temporal models are possible:

- sensitive-period model;
- recent-exposure model;
- accumulation model;
- pathway/cascade model.

Researchers should compare these models rather than assume one timing story.

## 35. Moral and social development

Moral reasoning, empathy, prosocial behavior and norm understanding develop through cognition, emotion, family interaction, peers and culture.

Classic stage theories are historically important but modern evidence emphasizes context and multidimensionality.

Xem [[08_moral_psychology_and_prosocial_behavior]].

## 36. Development and psychopathology

Mental disorder risk is developmental. Same symptom can mean different things at different ages; pathways can be equifinal and multifinal.

**Equifinality:** different pathways produce similar outcome.

**Multifinality:** same risk leads to different outcomes.

This is why diagnosis should consider developmental history, not just current symptom list.

## 37. Common misconceptions

### “Ba năm đầu quyết định cả đời”

Quá mạnh. Early experience matters, but later plasticity/intervention/context remain important.

### “Teen brain finishes at exactly 25”

Sai simplification.

### “Developmental stage applies universally”

No. Some stage concepts are useful historical/theoretical frameworks, not universal clocks.

### “High early risk means bad outcome inevitable”

No. Risk is probabilistic.

### “Aging means general intelligence collapses”

No. Cognitive domains change differently.

## 38. Mental model

```text
Biology
 + prior state
 + learning
 + relationships
 + culture
 + socioeconomic context
 + historical time
        ↓
Current adaptation
        ↓
behavior changes environment
        ↓
next developmental state
```

Development is path-dependent but not fate.
