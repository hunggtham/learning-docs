# Căng thẳng nghề nghiệp, burnout và phục hồi

**Burnout** nên được hiểu như một hiện tượng nghề nghiệp liên quan stress kéo dài trong bối cảnh công việc, không phải một explanation toàn năng cho mọi fatigue, depression hay dissatisfaction. Một người kiệt sức có thể đồng thời có sleep deprivation, depression, medical problem, caregiving burden hoặc financial stress; assessment cần tách các nguồn này.

> **Trạng thái bằng chứng tổng quát:** workload, low control, role conflict, unfairness, insufficient recovery và poor organizational support có association đáng kể với burnout. Burnout interventions có evidence nhưng effect heterogeneous; organizational intervention thường quan trọng vì cause không chỉ nằm ở individual coping.

## 1. Burnout không đơn giản là “mệt”

Các frameworks phổ biến thường mô tả burnout qua các dimensions như exhaustion, cynicism/depersonalization và reduced professional efficacy, nhưng exact measurement model thay instrument.

Burnout should not be inferred from one bad week.

## 2. Occupational context là core

Burnout concept specifically links suffering to work context. Nếu symptoms pervasive across all domains, differential assessment for depression, anxiety, sleep disorder or medical conditions becomes important.

Burnout and depression overlap but are not identical constructs.

## 3. Job Demands–Resources model

**Mô hình yêu cầu–nguồn lực công việc (Job Demands–Resources model)** proposes burnout risk rises when demands are high and resources insufficient.

Demands can include:

- workload;
- emotional labor;
- role conflict;
- interruptions;
- time pressure;
- difficult customers;
- cognitive overload.

Resources include:

- autonomy;
- support;
- feedback;
- staffing;
- role clarity;
- recovery opportunity;
- fair processes.

> **Current theory/model:** JD-R is useful organizing framework, not a universal causal law with one fixed equation.

## 4. Workload và control

High workload is not always harmful if time-limited, meaningful and accompanied by autonomy/recovery. Chronic uncontrollable demand is more problematic.

Control matters because same workload feels different when worker can sequence tasks, negotiate deadline or ask for help.

## 5. Effort–reward imbalance

When high effort is paired with low reward, low recognition or unfair return, stress can persist. Reward includes money but also status, security, feedback and perceived fairness.

This model captures important association, but individual/context differences still matter.

## 6. Organizational justice

**Công bằng tổ chức (organizational justice)** includes fairness of outcomes, procedures and interpersonal treatment.

Unfair decision-making can create chronic uncertainty and value conflict even when workload is moderate.

## 7. Burnout is not a personal-resilience failure

If five people in one team show escalating exhaustion after staffing cuts, framing problem as “they need mindfulness” ignores system cause.

Individual coping can help symptoms, but it does not repair impossible workload, abusive management or chronic understaffing.

## 8. Psychological safety

Low psychological safety can increase effort cost because people spend resources hiding uncertainty, avoiding questions and protecting image.

Psychological safety does not eliminate workload, but it can improve learning, error reporting and support-seeking.

## 9. Moral distress and moral injury

Some work stress comes from being unable to act according to professional values or repeatedly participating in decisions perceived as wrong.

This can produce guilt, shame, anger or betrayal that standard workload model does not fully capture.

Xem [[../90_connections/04_moral_injury_shame_guilt_and_value_conflict]].

## 10. Recovery

Recovery is not only “sleep enough”. Psychological detachment from work, rest, low-demand activity, social connection and restored sense of control can matter.

### Recovery paradox

People most depleted may have least capacity to initiate healthy recovery, instead doomscrolling, staying awake or using numbing behavior. Therefore environment design matters.

## 11. Sleep and burnout

Sleep loss increases emotional reactivity, attention problems and fatigue; burnout/stress can in turn disturb sleep.

Bidirectional loop:

```text
Work stress
→ arousal / late work
→ poor sleep
→ lower regulation / performance
→ more work stress
```

## 12. Rumination after work

Replaying unresolved task or conflict can keep physiological/cognitive activation high after working hours.

Rumination is not simply “thinking too much”; it often reflects unresolved goal, uncertainty or perceived injustice.

Interventions can target closure rituals, planning, boundary and cognitive strategy, but root work condition still matters.

## 13. Digital work and interruption

Chat, email and notification create fragmented attention and expectation of availability. Switching cost and unfinished-task cues can extend work into recovery time.

A culture that expects instant response may create chronic low-level vigilance even without explicit overtime.

## 14. Remote work

Remote work can improve autonomy and reduce commute, but may weaken boundaries, social support or belonging.

> **Evidence status:** effects are heterogeneous and depend on task, home environment, team practice, autonomy and individual preference. “Remote work causes burnout” is too broad.

## 15. Individual interventions

Examples include stress-management training, CBT-based techniques, mindfulness, relaxation, sleep intervention and exercise.

Evidence suggests some individual interventions can reduce burnout symptoms, but effect size and durability vary. They should not be treated as substitute for organizational change when demands are structural.

## 16. Organizational interventions

Potential strategies:

- workload/staffing redesign;
- schedule control;
- reducing unnecessary bureaucracy;
- role clarity;
- fair leadership;
- protected recovery;
- better team communication;
- decision participation.

Recent reviews support importance of organizational approaches, though intervention designs and populations remain heterogeneous.

## 17. Multilevel intervention

A strong model combines:

```text
Individual skills
+ team process
+ job design
+ organizational policy
```

No single level explains all burnout.

## 18. Measurement caveat

Burnout scores vary by instrument and cutoff. There is no universal blood test or one threshold that separates two natural populations.

Score should be interpreted with instrument validity, occupational context and impairment.

Xem [[../00_foundations/05_psychometrics_and_test_interpretation]].

## 19. Burnout vs depression

Overlap includes fatigue, low motivation and cognitive difficulty. Depression often extends beyond occupational context and includes broader mood/anhedonia patterns, but real cases can overlap.

A burnout label should not delay mental-health assessment when symptoms are severe or pervasive.

## 20. Everyday self-regulation boundary

Useful low-risk strategies include protecting sleep, reducing notification load, planning shutdown, asking for priority clarification and taking recovery breaks.

But library should state evidence level:

> These are mechanism-informed strategies, not guaranteed treatments for clinical or organizational burnout.

## 21. Common misconceptions

### “Burnout means weak resilience”

Sai. Organizational demands/resources play major roles.

### “Vacation cures burnout”

Temporary recovery may help, but unchanged system can recreate problem.

### “Mindfulness fixes burnout”

It may help some people, but cannot replace staffing/fairness/workload reform.

### “Burnout and depression are same thing”

They overlap but are not interchangeable constructs.

## 22. Mental model

```text
Job demands
 - resources/control/fairness
 + individual vulnerability
 + sleep/recovery
 + team climate
        ↓
Strain / burnout risk
        ↓
behavior & performance changes
        ↓
feedback into job demands
```
