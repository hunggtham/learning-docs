# Tâm lý trị liệu và cơ chế thay đổi

Tâm lý trị liệu (psychotherapy) không phải một kỹ thuật đơn lẻ. Nó là tập hợp nhiều can thiệp có mục tiêu thay đổi distress, impairment, behavior, emotion regulation, relationship pattern hoặc cách một người phản ứng với experience.

Điều quan trọng là phân biệt ba câu hỏi:

```text
Therapy có giúp không?
Therapy giúp ai, trong điều kiện nào?
Therapy thay đổi qua mechanism nào?
```

Một treatment có outcome evidence không có nghĩa mechanism đã được xác định chắc chắn.

> **Trạng thái bằng chứng tổng quát:** nhiều psychotherapies có efficacy/effectiveness evidence cho specific conditions. Alliance và một số process variables có robust association với outcome; causal mechanism của psychotherapy vẫn khó xác lập hoàn toàn vì mediation, timing và third-variable problems.

## 1. Efficacy, effectiveness và mechanism

**Hiệu lực trong nghiên cứu (efficacy)** hỏi treatment có benefit trong controlled trial không.

**Hiệu quả thực tế (effectiveness)** hỏi treatment hoạt động thế nào trong routine care với comorbidity, dropout và heterogeneous clinicians.

**Cơ chế thay đổi (mechanism of change)** hỏi process nào causally produces improvement.

Ba câu hỏi cần design khác nhau.

## 2. Outcome không chỉ là symptom score

Outcome có thể gồm:

- symptom reduction;
- functioning;
- quality of life;
- relationship;
- relapse;
- work/school participation;
- patient-defined goal;
- adverse effect;
- dropout.

Nếu study chỉ đo symptom ngay sau treatment, nó không trả lời toàn bộ clinical usefulness.

## 3. Treatment package vs ingredient

Một RCT thường test package gồm therapist contact, explanation, homework, exposure, cognitive work, expectation và attention.

Nếu package works, chưa biết ingredient nào necessary.

Dismantling studies, mediation studies và experimental manipulation needed to identify mechanism.

## 4. Common factors

**Yếu tố chung (common factors)** include alliance, empathy, expectancy, goal agreement và therapist responsiveness.

These factors repeatedly associate with outcome across modalities.

> **Established association:** alliance/outcome relation is robust.
>
> **Mechanism status:** association alone does not prove alliance causally drives all improvement. Early symptom change can improve alliance, and client/therapist variables may influence both.

## 5. Specific techniques

Some conditions show meaningful benefit from specific procedures: exposure for anxiety/OCD, behavioral activation for depression, skills training or family interventions in selected contexts.

Therefore “all therapies work only because relationship” is too strong.

Likewise “only technique matters” ignores therapist/client/context process.

## 6. Therapeutic alliance

Alliance often includes:

- bond;
- agreement on goals;
- agreement on tasks.

Strong alliance does not mean never challenging client. Productive disagreement can occur inside collaborative relationship.

Alliance rupture and repair may itself provide learning opportunity.

## 7. Expectancy and rationale

Believing treatment is credible can affect engagement, attention and persistence.

But expectancy effects should not be confused with “symptom is imaginary”. Expectation can modulate real perception, pain, motivation and behavior.

Xem [[../06_applied/13_placebo_nocebo_expectation_and_context]].

## 8. Learning as a broad change principle

Many therapies can be understood partly as learning:

```text
Old prediction
→ new experience
→ prediction error
→ updated response
```

Exposure challenges threat prediction; behavioral activation challenges withdrawal/reward patterns; interpersonal work can update relationship expectations.

This is useful cross-school model but still a **current framework**, not one proven universal mechanism for every therapy.

## 9. Exposure and inhibitory learning

Exposure does not simply erase fear. Modern models often emphasize new safety/non-threat learning competing with old association.

Context variation, expectancy violation and reduction of safety behavior can support generalization.

Xem [[01_cbt_behavioral_and_third_wave]].

## 10. Cognitive change

Changing appraisal or belief can affect emotion and behavior. But cognitive change may be cause, mediator, consequence or correlate of symptom improvement depending treatment.

Mediation requires temporal evidence and control of confounding, not just pre-post correlation.

## 11. Emotion regulation

Many treatments help clients identify, tolerate, reappraise or change response to emotion.

Meta-analytic process research finds some emotional change variables associated with outcome, but mechanisms are difficult to establish because process and symptom often change together.

> **Evidence status:** association is stronger than causal proof for many proposed mechanisms.

## 12. Psychological flexibility

ACT and some integrative models emphasize ability to remain in contact with experience while choosing behavior aligned with values.

Psychological flexibility is an influential current construct with substantial research, but it should not be treated as proven single mechanism behind all effective psychotherapy.

## 13. Behavioral activation

Behavioral activation targets avoidance/inactivity loops and increases contact with meaningful/rewarding activity.

Its evidence for depression supports behavior-focused change, but “just be active” is not equivalent to structured treatment.

## 14. Skills acquisition

DBT and other structured therapies teach emotion regulation, distress tolerance, mindfulness and interpersonal skills.

Skill knowledge alone is insufficient; repeated practice and context generalization matter.

## 15. Psychodynamic process

Modern psychodynamic treatments may target recurring relationship patterns, affect avoidance, defense-like processes and self-understanding.

Outcome evidence for psychodynamic treatment does not validate all classical Freud claims.

Xem [[03_psychodynamic_humanistic_and_systemic_therapy]].

## 16. Humanistic process

Empathy, congruence and acceptance can reduce interpersonal threat and support exploration.

These relational conditions are clinically important but not a complete substitute for disorder-specific intervention when one exists.

## 17. Systemic change

For couple/family problems, reciprocal interaction patterns can maintain distress. Changing communication, contingencies, role or boundary may alter system loop.

Systemic explanation should not become family blame.

## 18. Measurement-Based Care

**Chăm sóc dựa trên đo lường (Measurement-Based Care — MBC)** repeatedly measures symptom/function and feeds data into clinical decision.

Potential benefits:

- detect nonresponse early;
- identify deterioration;
- improve communication;
- support treatment adjustment;
- reduce reliance on therapist intuition alone.

Recent reviews support clinical value, though implementation burden, measure choice and user acceptance matter.

## 19. Routine outcome monitoring

Therapist judgment alone can miss deterioration. Repeated validated measures provide additional signal.

But score should not replace conversation. Measurement is decision support, not person.

Xem [[../00_foundations/05_psychometrics_and_test_interpretation]].

## 20. Deterioration and adverse effects

Not everyone improves. Some clients deteriorate, experience increased distress, dependency concerns, relationship disruption or treatment burden.

Evidence-based practice includes monitoring harm, not only average benefit.

## 21. Dropout

Dropout is not always treatment failure: client may improve, dislike treatment, face cost/logistics or choose alternative care.

Still, high dropout can reduce real-world effectiveness and signal poor fit.

## 22. Therapist effects

Outcome varies between therapists even within same treatment model.

This suggests competence, responsiveness, alliance skill, case mix and other therapist factors matter.

But ranking therapists without adequate risk adjustment can be misleading.

## 23. Treatment adherence and competence

Strict manual adherence can preserve intervention integrity but excessive rigidity may reduce responsiveness.

Competence means using principle skillfully, not merely following steps.

Research on adherence-outcome relation is mixed across therapies.

## 24. Personalized treatment

The goal “choose best therapy for this individual” is scientifically attractive but difficult. Many moderator findings do not replicate or have small effect.

> **Current goal/hypothesis space:** precision mental health is active research; current evidence rarely allows deterministic matching from a few traits.

## 25. Stepped care

Stepped care starts with least intensive evidence-based option appropriate to severity/risk, then increases intensity if needed.

This can improve resource allocation but requires monitoring and clear escalation criteria.

## 26. Combined treatment

Psychotherapy and medication can be combined depending condition, severity, preference and guideline.

“Medication vs therapy” is often false dichotomy.

Xem [[02_biological_and_community_treatment]].

## 27. Cultural adaptation

Language, explanatory model, family role, stigma and treatment expectation influence engagement.

Cultural adaptation should preserve active treatment principle while fitting context; it is not simply translating worksheets.

## 28. Evidence-based practice is broader than RCT

Clinical decision integrates:

```text
Best available research evidence
+ clinician expertise
+ patient preference/values
+ context/resources
+ ongoing outcome data
```

This does not mean “anything goes”. Preference cannot turn unsupported treatment into evidence-based treatment, but it matters when selecting among reasonable options.

## 29. Mechanism research is hard

To establish mediator as causal mechanism, ideally show:

1. treatment changes mediator;
2. mediator changes before outcome;
3. mediator predicts outcome change;
4. alternative cause is addressed;
5. experimental manipulation supports pathway.

Many psychotherapy mechanism studies do not satisfy all conditions.

Therefore chapter should use phrases like “associated mechanism” or “candidate mechanism” when causal evidence incomplete.

## 30. Common misconceptions

### “Nếu therapy works, theory behind it is true”

Sai.

### “All therapies are equally effective for everything”

Sai. Condition-specific evidence differs.

### “Alliance is all that matters”

Too strong.

### “Manualized therapy ignores individuality”

Not necessarily. Manual can specify principle while therapist adapts delivery.

### “Psychotherapy cannot cause harm”

False. Deterioration/adverse effects can occur and should be monitored.

## 31. Mental model

```text
Assessment
   ↓
Case formulation
   ↓
Treatment selection
   ↓
Specific + common processes
   ↓
Repeated measurement
   ↓
Improve / no response / deteriorate
   ↓
Adapt plan
```

Psychotherapy is an iterative learning system, not one-time application of theory.
