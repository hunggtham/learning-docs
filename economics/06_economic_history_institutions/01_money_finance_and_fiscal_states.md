# Money, Finance & Fiscal States — Từ coinage đến banking, public debt và financial deepening

Economic history của money và finance là history của trust, accounting và institutions. Financial development mở rộng khả năng chuyển resources qua thời gian và giữa người với người, nhưng cũng tạo leverage, maturity mismatch và crisis risk.

Module này không kể chronology của từng currency. Nó tập trung vào mechanisms làm monetary/financial institutions thay đổi trade, state capacity và growth.

## 1. Money as institution

Money hoạt động khi users tin unit sẽ được accept và purchasing power đủ predictable.

Commodity content có thể hỗ trợ trust nhưng không phải điều kiện duy nhất. State taxation, legal tender, banking networks và social convention đều tạo monetary demand.

## 2. Coinage and seigniorage

States historically gained seigniorage by minting coin or issuing currency.

Debasement can raise short-run fiscal resources but may weaken trust and alter prices/contracts.

Not every change in metallic content maps one-for-one to inflation because velocity, supply and confidence matter.

## 3. Unit of account vs medium

Accounts can be denominated in one unit while settlement uses another asset.

This distinction helps explain monetary systems with bills, coins, deposits and clearing networks operating together.

## 4. Bills of exchange

Long-distance trade expanded through transferable claims that reduced need to ship specie.

Bills combined credit, payment and FX functions, relying on merchant networks and reputation.

## 5. Merchant banking

Merchant-bank networks lowered information and enforcement costs across borders.

Relationship finance solved asymmetric information but concentrated access within networks.

## 6. Deposit banking and clearing

Banks economize on settlement by netting claims rather than moving physical currency for every transaction.

Clearing institutions increase liquidity efficiency but create interbank dependence.

## 7. Fractional banking and runs

Banks fund illiquid assets with more liquid liabilities. This maturity transformation supports investment but creates run vulnerability.

Historical crises reveal why lender-of-last-resort and deposit insurance evolved.

## 8. Central banking origins

Central banks often emerged from a mix of fiscal finance, note issuance, settlement and crisis-management functions.

Their modern price-stability mandate is historically contingent, not the only possible institutional purpose.

## 9. Lender of last resort

Classic logic provides liquidity against good collateral to solvent institutions during panic.

Historical difficulty is real-time distinction between illiquidity and insolvency.

## 10. Public debt markets

Reliable tax capacity and credible repayment support long-term sovereign borrowing.

Deep public debt markets create benchmark yield curves and safe assets, helping private finance.

## 11. Fiscal-military state

War historically pushed states to improve taxation, administration and borrowing capacity.

Military competition can strengthen fiscal capacity, but also impose destructive debt/tax burdens.

State-building effect depends institutions and survival incentives.

## 12. Financial repression

Governments can direct banks to hold public debt, cap interest rates or restrict capital movement.

This lowers funding costs but can distort capital allocation and reduce saver returns.

## 13. Banking vs market-based finance

Bank-oriented systems rely more on relationship lending; market-based systems use bonds/equity more heavily.

Neither architecture is universally superior. Industry structure, legal enforcement and stage of development matter.

## 14. Joint-stock companies

Limited liability and tradable shares pool capital from many investors and separate ownership/control.

This expands scale but creates agency problems and need for accounting/governance.

## 15. Accounting and disclosure

Standardized accounts reduce information asymmetry and support outside investment.

Auditing, disclosure and securities law are financial infrastructure, not bureaucratic add-ons.

## 16. Limited liability

Limited liability caps investor downside, encouraging diversified equity investment.

It also shifts some tail risk to creditors/society, motivating capital requirements and tort/regulatory rules.

## 17. Collateral and land registries

Reliable ownership records turn assets into collateral, potentially expanding credit.

But collateral-based finance can exclude productive borrowers without wealth and amplify asset-price cycles.

## 18. Mortgage finance

Long-duration housing finance increases home access but exposes households/banks to rate and house-price risk.

Institutional differences in fixed vs variable rates shape monetary transmission.

## 19. Financial deepening

More credit/GDP or financial assets can reflect better intermediation, but beyond a point leverage/misallocation may rise.

Quantity of finance is not synonymous with quality.

## 20. Credit booms

Credit booms can finance productive investment or asset speculation.

Warning signs include rapid leverage, weak underwriting, maturity/currency mismatch and asset-price feedback.

## 21. Asset bubbles

Bubble claims are hard to identify in real time because fundamentals are uncertain.

Historical episodes should be analyzed through leverage, beliefs, financing and forced-sale mechanisms rather than retrospective labels alone.

## 22. Bank crises

Losses reduce equity; funding withdrawals force fire sales; price declines create mark-to-market losses elsewhere.

Network/interconnectedness can turn institution-specific shock systemic.

## 23. Sovereign-bank doom loop

Banks hold sovereign bonds; sovereign may backstop banks.

Bank losses weaken government finances, sovereign stress weakens bank balance sheets, creating feedback.

## 24. Currency crises and original sin

Foreign-currency debt makes depreciation worsen balance sheets.

Historical development of local-currency bond markets reduces one risk but does not eliminate capital-flow volatility.

## 25. Gold standard

Gold convertibility can anchor long-run nominal expectations but restrict monetary response and transmit external adjustment through prices/output/reserves.

Its performance depends on credibility, wage flexibility, capital flows and political tolerance for domestic adjustment.

## 26. Bretton Woods-type regimes

Fixed-but-adjustable exchange systems trade monetary autonomy against capital controls and international coordination.

They illustrate macro trilemma institutionally rather than only theoretically.

## 27. Fiat money

Fiat systems replace commodity convertibility with institutional credibility, taxation capacity and monetary policy frameworks.

Trust shifts from metal constraint to governance rule.

## 28. Inflation and fiscal regimes

High inflation episodes often involve monetary accommodation, fiscal stress, supply shocks and credibility breakdown in varying proportions.

Historical diagnosis requires balance-sheet and institutional context, not “money supply” alone.

## 29. Financial regulation cycle

Crises often lead to tighter capital/liquidity/disclosure rules; long stability can encourage deregulation or regulatory arbitrage.

Regulation evolves with financial innovation.

## 30. Shadow banking

Credit intermediation outside traditional deposit banks can reduce costs and diversify funding but reproduce maturity/liquidity transformation without same safeguards.

Institutional perimeter matters more than legal label “bank”.

## 31. Payment technology

From clearinghouses to cards and instant payments, lower transaction cost expands exchange and changes bank/platform roles.

Network effects can create concentration in payment infrastructure.

## 32. Financial inclusion

Access to payments/savings/credit can improve resilience and investment, but predatory lending or unsuitable debt can harm households.

Inclusion quality matters more than account count.

## 33. Historical identification

Financial reforms often occur after crises, making before-after comparisons endogenous.

Credible designs may exploit staggered branch deregulation, geographic bank access, historical networks or policy thresholds.

## 34. Failure modes

Sai lầm thứ nhất là equate more credit with better development.

Sai lầm thứ hai là treat every historical panic as same mechanism.

Sai lầm thứ ba là infer causality from finance-growth correlation.

Sai lầm thứ tư là describe central bank history as linear evolution toward one inevitable model.

Sai lầm thứ năm là ignore fiscal institutions behind monetary credibility.

## 35. Analysis template

Khi phân tích financial institution/history, hãy hỏi:

1. liability nào được coi là money/liquid claim?
2. trust/enforcement đến từ đâu?
3. maturity/currency mismatch ở đâu?
4. state tax/borrowing capacity liên quan thế nào?
5. crisis amplification channel là run, fire sale, leverage hay sovereign link?
6. reform thay incentive nào?
7. distribution of credit access thay đổi ra sao?
8. regulation tạo arbitrage mới không?
9. historical policy có endogenous response to crisis không?
10. identification strategy nào tách institution effect khỏi broader development?

Financial institutions mở rộng khả năng mobilize capital. Chapter tiếp theo đặt chúng cùng technology, energy, organization và trade để hiểu industrialization và modern productivity growth.
