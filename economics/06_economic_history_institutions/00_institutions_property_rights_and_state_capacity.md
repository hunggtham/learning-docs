# Institutions, Property Rights & State Capacity — Rules, enforcement và khả năng tổ chức nền kinh tế

Economic History & Institutions không kể lại chronology của World History. Mục tiêu ở đây là dùng lịch sử để hiểu vì sao cùng một technology, resource endowment hoặc trade opportunity có thể tạo kết quả khác nhau dưới institutional arrangements khác nhau.

Institution (`제도`) là tập rules, enforcement mechanisms và expectations định hình interaction. Nó có thể formal như constitution, tax code, courts; hoặc informal như norms, reputation và local governance. Institution không chỉ là “government quality” và cũng không thể đo đầy đủ bằng một index duy nhất.

## 1. Rules và enforcement phải tách riêng

Hai countries có cùng commercial law trên giấy nhưng enforcement khác nhau có thể tạo outcomes rất khác.

Do đó institutional analysis cần hỏi:

```text
Rule là gì?
Ai enforce?
Violation bị phát hiện thế nào?
Penalty có credible không?
Actors kỳ vọng rule tồn tại bao lâu?
```

De jure rules và de facto institutions không luôn trùng nhau.

## 2. Property rights

Property rights gồm quyền use, exclude, transfer và hưởng residual returns.

Secure rights có thể khuyến khích investment bằng cách tăng expected return và collateral value.

Nhưng property-right reform cũng thay đổi distribution of bargaining power; formalization không tự động Pareto-improving.

## 3. Contract enforcement

Complex specialization cần parties tin contract sẽ được enforce hoặc repeated relationships đủ mạnh.

Weak enforcement làm firms vertical-integrate nhiều hơn, rely on family/network ties hoặc avoid long-term investment.

Thus transaction costs alter organizational form.

## 4. Transaction costs

Transaction costs gồm search, bargaining, monitoring, verification và enforcement.

Institutions matter partly because they lower or redistribute these costs.

A market does not exist independently of institutional infrastructure.

## 5. State capacity

State capacity thường có nhiều dimensions:

```text
fiscal capacity
administrative capacity
legal/enforcement capacity
information capacity
coercive/security capacity
```

Một state có thể mạnh ở một dimension nhưng yếu ở dimension khác.

## 6. Fiscal capacity

Khả năng tax rộng và predictably cho phép finance public goods, defense, infrastructure và administration.

But taxation can be extractive if accountability/representation weak.

The same capacity can support development or predation depending political institutions.

## 7. Information capacity

Census, land records, firm registries và standardized accounting giúp state tax, regulate và provide services.

Information infrastructure is a production technology for governance.

Digitalization can improve collection but also expand surveillance power, so institutional checks matter.

## 8. Bureaucratic capability

Professional recruitment, career incentives và administrative continuity can improve implementation.

But bureaucracy can become rigid or captured. Capability is not simply number of civil servants.

## 9. Legal origins and path dependence

Legal systems inherit rules from conquest, colonization, transplantation and domestic reform.

Historical legal origin may correlate with finance/labor regulation, but causal interpretation is contested because legal families also proxy broader history.

## 10. Inclusive vs extractive framing

A useful conceptual contrast asks whether institutions broaden participation/investment incentives or concentrate rents and block entry.

But binary labels are too coarse for empirical work. Real institutions mix inclusive and extractive features across sectors/time.

## 11. Credible commitment

Governments need ability to commit not to confiscate returns after investment.

Independent courts, constitutional constraints, representative bodies or reputation can reduce expropriation risk.

Credible commitment can lower borrowing cost and encourage long-horizon investment.

## 12. Sovereign borrowing and institutions

States borrow based on expected repayment capacity and willingness.

Historical development of parliaments, tax systems and debt markets often co-evolved: creditors lend more when future taxation and repayment rules become credible.

## 13. Limited access orders

Some political economies maintain stability by restricting access to valuable organizations/markets and sharing rents among elites.

Opening entry can increase competition but threaten political equilibrium, explaining why inefficient restrictions persist.

## 14. Rent-seeking

When policy creates scarce licenses, monopoly rights or subsidies, actors spend resources competing for rents rather than producing new value.

Rent-seeking cost can exceed visible transfer.

## 15. Corruption as mechanism-specific problem

“Corruption” can mean bribery, embezzlement, patronage, procurement collusion or regulatory capture.

Each has different incentives and remedies.

Broad perception indices are descriptive; mechanism-specific evidence is needed for causal claims.

## 16. Informal institutions

Trust, kinship, norms and reputation can substitute for weak courts.

They can enable trade within networks but limit anonymous exchange or exclude outsiders.

Development often changes the balance between relational contracting and impersonal institutions.

## 17. Collective action

Public-good provision depends on group size, monitoring, local knowledge and sanctioning.

Historical commons and irrigation systems show communities can govern resources without pure privatization or centralized control.

## 18. Institutional complementarity

One reform may fail if complementary institutions absent.

Property title without functioning courts or credit market may not raise investment. Competition law without regulatory capacity may remain symbolic.

## 19. Institutional persistence

Institutions can persist because they create organizations, vested interests and expectations that reinforce them.

Persistence does not mean immutability; shocks can change relative power and make reform possible.

## 20. Critical junctures

Wars, revolutions, crises, regime changes and technological shifts can weaken old coalitions and create institutional openings.

Calling an event a critical juncture requires showing it changed path, not simply that it was historically dramatic.

## 21. Historical identification problem

Institutions are endogenous: richer societies may demand better institutions while institutions also influence growth.

Cross-country correlation therefore cannot establish direction.

Historical studies use borders, lotteries, colonial rules, geographic discontinuities, settlement patterns or reforms — all with contested exclusion assumptions.

## 22. Persistence designs

A common design links historical treatment to modern outcomes.

Credibility requires:

```text
historical assignment plausibly exogenous
mechanism of persistence
no alternative persistent channels dominating
measurement comparable over time
```

Long time horizons increase scope for confounding.

## 23. Geography vs institutions

Geography affects disease, agriculture, transport and resource access. Institutions can mediate these effects.

The useful question is often interaction, not “geography or institutions?” as mutually exclusive explanations.

## 24. Distribution of power

Economic institutions reflect political power. Asset ownership, military organization, voting rights and elite cohesion influence which rules are chosen.

A welfare-improving reform can be blocked if it reduces rents of decisive groups.

## 25. De jure vs de facto political power

Formal voting rights differ from practical influence through wealth, organization, media or coercion.

Institutional analysis should not infer power only from constitutional text.

## 26. Failure modes

Sai lầm thứ nhất là treat institution index as the institution itself.

Sai lầm thứ hai là assume formal rule implies enforcement.

Sai lầm thứ ba là call any persistent difference “path dependence”.

Sai lầm thứ tư là use historical correlation as proof without assignment mechanism.

Sai lầm thứ năm là treat state capacity as universally good independent of accountability.

## 27. Analysis template

Khi phân tích một institution, hãy hỏi:

1. rule và enforcement mechanism cụ thể là gì?
2. actor nào có power và rents?
3. transaction cost nào institution giảm/tăng?
4. investment/entry/contract incentive thay đổi thế nào?
5. fiscal/information/legal capacity nào cần để implement?
6. distribution winners/losers là ai?
7. complementary institutions nào thiếu?
8. persistence mechanism là organization, norm hay asset distribution?
9. historical variation nào identify effect?
10. geography/technology/politics alternative channels là gì?

Institutional foundation này dẫn sang money, finance và fiscal systems: những institutions cho phép societies huy động savings, fund states và phân bổ capital qua thời gian.
