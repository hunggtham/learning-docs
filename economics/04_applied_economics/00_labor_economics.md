# Labor Economics — Human capital, labor supply, wage setting và labor-market power

Labor economics áp dụng microeconomics, information economics, market power và econometrics vào một market đặc biệt: người lao động không chỉ bán “một commodity” mà lựa chọn giữa work, leisure, location, occupation, training, family responsibilities và future career paths. Firm cũng không chỉ “mua labor”; họ tuyển, sàng lọc, đào tạo, giữ chân và thiết kế incentive trong điều kiện information không hoàn hảo.

Vì vậy wage không thể được hiểu chỉ như intersection của một supply curve và demand curve tĩnh. Nó phản ánh productivity, bargaining, search frictions, outside options, institutions và market power.

## 1. Labor demand là derived demand

Firm thuê labor vì labor tạo output có market value. Trong competitive output/labor benchmark, firm thuê đến khi:

```text
Value of Marginal Product of Labor = Wage
```

Nếu output price `P` và marginal product of labor `MPL`:

```text
VMPL = P × MPL
```

Labor demand dịch chuyển khi productivity, output demand, capital stock hoặc technology thay đổi.

## 2. Substitution và scale effects

Khi wage tăng, firm có thể thay labor bằng capital/software — substitution effect.

Nhưng wage tăng cũng tăng production cost, làm output giảm — scale effect — kéo labor demand xuống thêm.

Technology vì vậy không đơn giản “replace jobs”; nó có thể substitute một task nhưng complement các tasks khác và làm output scale tăng.

## 3. Task-based view của automation

Occupation là bundle of tasks. Technology có thể automate routine task, augment analytical task hoặc tạo task mới.

Do đó phân tích automation nên hỏi:

```text
Which tasks are substitutable?
Which tasks become more productive?
Does demand for final output expand?
What new tasks emerge?
```

Đây là tốt hơn câu hỏi nhị phân “job này có biến mất không?”.

## 4. Labor supply và leisure

Individual allocates time giữa work và non-market time.

Wage tăng có hai effects:

- substitution effect: leisure đắt hơn → work nhiều hơn;
- income effect: người giàu hơn có thể demand leisure nhiều hơn → work ít hơn.

Net labor-supply response là empirical question.

## 5. Extensive vs intensive margin

Labor supply thay đổi qua:

```text
Extensive margin → có tham gia labor force / có làm việc hay không
Intensive margin → nếu làm thì bao nhiêu giờ
```

Tax/childcare/pension policy có thể tác động hai margins rất khác nhau.

## 6. Reservation wage

Reservation wage là mức wage thấp nhất khiến worker chấp nhận job, phản ánh unemployment benefits, savings, family income, commuting cost, job conditions và search expectations.

Nó không phải một fixed psychological number; institutions và household balance sheet làm nó thay đổi.

## 7. Human capital

Education, training, health và experience có thể tăng productivity, từ đó tăng wage.

Mincer-style earnings equation thường viết:

```text
log(wage) = α + β schooling + γ experience + δ experience² + u
```

Nhưng `β` không tự động causal return to schooling vì ability/family background ảnh hưởng cả schooling và wages.

## 8. Signaling vs skill formation

Education có thể tăng productivity thật và đồng thời signal pre-existing traits.

Empirical design cần variation như compulsory-schooling laws, admission thresholds, lotteries hoặc institutional changes để tách selection.

“Education is signaling” hoặc “education is pure human capital” đều là conclusions quá mạnh nếu không có evidence context-specific.

## 9. Compensating wage differentials

Jobs khó chịu/nguy hiểm có thể phải trả wage cao hơn để attract workers, all else equal.

Nhưng observed dangerous jobs đôi khi trả thấp vì worker sorting, weak bargaining hoặc limited alternatives.

Do đó cross-sectional wage gap không trực tiếp đo willingness to accept risk.

## 10. Search and matching

Workers và firms cần thời gian tìm match. Search frictions tạo unemployment và wage dispersion ngay cả giữa workers có productivity tương tự.

Matching quality ảnh hưởng turnover và productivity.

## 11. Job ladder

Workers thường move từ low-wage/low-productivity firms sang better matches theo thời gian.

Job-to-job transitions là key channel của wage growth. Recession làm job ladder chậm lại dù unemployment không tăng mạnh.

## 12. Monopsony

Monopsony không nhất thiết nghĩa chỉ có một employer. Nếu workers face search frictions, moving costs, non-compete clauses, location constraints hoặc imperfect information, mỗi firm có upward-sloping labor supply.

Firm có wage-setting power:

```text
Marginal labor expense > wage
```

và có thể hire fewer workers at lower wage than competitive benchmark.

## 13. Minimum wage under different models

Trong competitive labor market, binding minimum wage có thể giảm employment.

Trong monopsony, moderate minimum wage có thể tăng cả wage và employment bằng cách hạn chế employer wage-setting power.

Therefore sign/magnitude is empirical and depends on market structure, policy level, adjustment margins, prices, hours, turnover and firm entry/exit.

## 14. Wage incidence

Payroll tax legally charged to employer may ultimately reduce wages or employment depending labor supply/demand elasticities.

Legal payer ≠ economic burden.

## 15. Unions và collective bargaining

Union có thể tăng worker bargaining power, compress wage dispersion và improve conditions. Nó cũng có thể raise labor cost, change employment or productivity depending institution.

Union effects depend on bargaining level, coverage, firm rents, product-market competition and work rules.

## 16. Efficiency wages

Firms may pay above outside wage to reduce turnover, attract quality, improve effort or strengthen morale.

This can generate wage dispersion and involuntary unemployment without pure monopsony.

## 17. Internal labor markets

Promotion ladders, seniority and firm-specific capital mean wage can reflect career contract rather than current marginal product each period.

Delayed compensation can create retention incentives but also lock-in.

## 18. Discrimination

Taste-based discrimination models prejudice as a preference/cost. Statistical discrimination arises when firms use group information as proxy under imperfect individual information.

Observed wage gap is not automatically discrimination because composition, occupation, hours, experience and selection matter. Conversely, “controlled gap” is not automatically clean causal measure because controls may themselves reflect past discrimination.

## 19. Audit studies

Resume correspondence experiments randomize names/signals across otherwise identical applications to estimate discrimination at hiring callback margin.

They have strong internal validity for that stage but do not measure promotion, wages after hire or long-run career discrimination.

## 20. Gender wage gap decomposition

Decomposition separates observed wage difference into parts associated with measured characteristics and residual structure.

Residual is not pure discrimination; it can contain unobserved productivity, measurement error, sorting and discrimination.

A decomposition is descriptive, not causal proof.

## 21. Occupational segregation

Workers sort across occupations due to skills, norms, constraints, networks, discrimination and preferences. Wage inequality therefore reflects both within-occupation and between-occupation mechanisms.

Changes in technology/trade can alter returns to task bundles.

## 22. Returns to experience

Wage often rises with experience then flattens, due to human capital, matching, promotions and selection.

Career interruptions can reduce accumulated experience, network and future wage growth.

## 23. Migration

Migration changes labor supply and can affect native wages/employment through substitutability/complementarity, capital adjustment, demand expansion and geography.

A local inflow also increases consumer demand and may induce firm entry, so partial-equilibrium labor supply story is incomplete.

## 24. Place-based shocks

Trade/technology shocks can persist geographically because workers do not instantly move. Housing, family ties, skills and local amenities create mobility frictions.

This links labor economics to regional/spatial economics.

## 25. Informality

In developing economies, informal employment affects tax coverage, benefits, firm size and productivity.

Workers may choose informality, be excluded from formal sector, or switch by lifecycle. Formalization policy affects margins beyond worker registration.

## 26. Gig/platform work

Platforms reduce matching/search costs but may centralize algorithmic control over prices, rankings and access.

Classification as employee/contractor changes insurance, taxes and bargaining institutions; economic effects depend on outside options and platform competition.

## 27. Empirical identification examples

Labor economics commonly uses:

- schooling-law IV/RDD for returns to education;
- minimum-wage border/event designs;
- plant closure as labor-demand shock;
- trade-shock exposure by region;
- hiring audit experiments;
- payroll-tax reforms;
- maternity/parental-leave policy DiD;
- lotteries for migration/visa programs.

Each design identifies a local/context-specific effect; do not universalize one estimate.

## 28. Estimands to distinguish

Questions include:

```text
Effect of one extra year of schooling on wages
Effect of minimum wage on employment/hours/prices
Effect of unemployment insurance on job-finding duration and match quality
Effect of parental leave on employment and earnings
Effect of immigration on native labor-market outcomes
```

Different outcomes and populations imply different estimands.

## 29. Equilibrium effects

A training program may raise wages for participants partly by competing for jobs with nonparticipants. A national scale-up can change wage structure, employer demand and training supply.

Micro treatment effect is not automatically general-equilibrium policy effect.

## 30. Distribution

Average employment effect can hide gains/losses by age, skill, gender, occupation, firm size and region.

Labor policy almost always has heterogeneous incidence.

## 31. Failure modes

Sai lầm thứ nhất là interpret wage as pure productivity.

Sai lầm thứ hai là infer causal return to education from raw wage-schooling correlation.

Sai lầm thứ ba là assume minimum wage effect sign from one benchmark model without market structure.

Sai lầm thứ tư là treat residual wage gap as exact discrimination measure.

Sai lầm thứ năm là ignore hours, turnover, prices and firm entry when looking only employment headcount.

## 32. Applied analysis template

Khi phân tích labor question, hãy đi theo thứ tự:

1. labor demand/supply/search/bargaining mechanism nào quan trọng?
2. market có competitive, monopsony hay matching friction?
3. outcome: wage, employment, hours, turnover, participation hay productivity?
4. treatment/policy exposure là gì?
5. selection/endogeneity ở đâu?
6. identification design nào plausible?
7. incidence rơi vào workers, firms hay consumers?
8. adjustment short run vs long run khác nhau không?
9. heterogeneity theo skill/group/region thế nào?
10. scaling có equilibrium effect nào?

Labor economics là applied layer đầu tiên: theory chỉ giúp xác định mechanisms; econometrics quyết định effect nào thật sự được identify. Public economics mở rộng logic này sang taxation, redistribution, insurance và government provision.
