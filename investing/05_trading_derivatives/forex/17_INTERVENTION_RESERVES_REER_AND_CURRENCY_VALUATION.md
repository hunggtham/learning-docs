# 17 — FX intervention, reserves, REER và currency valuation

Một currency có thể rẻ/đắt theo một valuation framework nhưng vẫn đi ngược view trong thời gian dài. Central bank có thể can thiệp nhưng không phải lúc nào cũng đảo trend. Reserve stock có thể lớn nhưng không đồng nghĩa usable liquidity vô hạn. Chapter này nối **valuation → external balance → intervention → reserves → regime** để tránh dùng một chỉ số đơn lẻ như trading signal.

Mental model:

```text
Relative prices / productivity / inflation
+ external balance
+ capital flows
+ policy regime
+ reserves / intervention
+ risk premium
→ medium-run currency pressure
```

## 1. Valuation khác timing

Currency valuation hỏi gần:

```text
Is the exchange rate expensive or cheap relative to a model/reference?
```

Trading timing hỏi:

```text
When and why will price converge, if at all?
```

Hai câu hỏi khác nhau.

Một currency có thể undervalued nhiều năm nếu productivity, capital controls, risk premium hoặc policy regime giữ equilibrium khác textbook anchor.

## 2. Nominal exchange rate

Nominal exchange rate là price của one currency in terms of another.

Ví dụ:

```text
USD/KRW
EUR/USD
USD/VND
```

Nominal move không tự nói competitiveness nếu domestic/foreign price levels thay đổi khác nhau.

## 3. Real exchange rate

Simplified bilateral real exchange rate concept:

```text
Real FX ≈ Nominal FX × Foreign Price Level / Domestic Price Level
```

Exact interpretation phụ thuộc quote convention.

Core idea: real exchange rate adjusts nominal FX for relative prices.

## 4. Real Effective Exchange Rate — REER

**REER** là trade-weighted real exchange-rate index against a basket of trading partners.

Nó kết hợp:

```text
bilateral exchange rates
× trade weights
× relative price/cost indices
```

REER useful for broad competitiveness/valuation discussion nhưng không phải executable price.

## 5. NEER vs REER

**Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER)** aggregates nominal bilateral rates.

**REER** adds relative inflation/cost adjustment.

If nominal currency appreciates but domestic inflation is lower than partners, real appreciation may be smaller than nominal move suggests.

## 6. Index level không có universal fair value

A REER index often normalized to base period such as 100.

`REER = 110` không tự nghĩa “10% overvalued”. Need know:

- base period;
- methodology;
- weights;
- price index;
- structural change.

Fair-value inference requires model/history, not raw index arithmetic.

## 7. Purchasing Power Parity — PPP

PPP intuition says same tradable basket should not sustain arbitrarily different currency-adjusted prices after frictions.

Absolute PPP is too strong for many real settings because:

- baskets differ;
- taxes/transport differ;
- services/nontradables matter;
- quality differs;
- trade barriers exist.

Relative PPP focuses on inflation differentials and long-run exchange-rate changes.

## 8. PPP works better as anchor than timer

Large deviations can persist.

Reasons include:

- productivity differences;
- capital flows;
- risk premium;
- terms of trade;
- nontradable prices;
- policy regime.

So:

```text
PPP cheap
≠ buy now
```

## 9. Balassa–Samuelson intuition

Fast productivity growth in tradable sectors can raise wages economy-wide. Nontradable prices may rise, producing real appreciation without loss of tradable competitiveness in the simple sense.

Thus richer/faster-productivity economies need not converge to identical absolute price levels.

## 10. Terms of trade and equilibrium FX

Commodity exporter receiving better export prices can experience stronger real income and potentially currency appreciation pressure.

Commodity importer hit by energy shock may face opposite pressure.

But flow response depends on hedging, fiscal saving, reserve policy and capital flows.

## 11. Current account as signal, not verdict

Current-account surplus may support external resilience but does not guarantee currency appreciation.

Residents can recycle surplus abroad; pension funds can buy foreign assets; central bank can accumulate reserves.

Accounting identity constrains flows but does not determine spot direction mechanically.

## 12. Net International Investment Position — NIIP

NIIP compares external financial assets and liabilities.

A debtor country with large foreign-currency liabilities may be more vulnerable to currency depreciation through balance-sheet effects.

But composition matters:

- FDI equity vs short-term debt;
- local-currency vs foreign-currency debt;
- maturity;
- hedging.

## 13. Gross positions matter too

Two countries with same net position can have very different gross cross-border assets/liabilities.

Large gross books create:

- hedging flows;
- valuation effects;
- funding risk;
- rebalancing.

Net data can hide these channels.

## 14. Reserve assets

Foreign-exchange reserves are external assets controlled by monetary authorities, usually including liquid foreign-currency assets and related reserve components according to official definitions.

They support functions such as:

- FX liquidity;
- intervention capacity;
- confidence;
- external-payment resilience.

## 15. Reserve size needs denominator

Absolute reserves alone are hard to interpret.

Common lenses include reserves relative to:

- imports;
- short-term external debt;
- broad money;
- external financing needs.

No single ratio is universal.

## 16. Usable reserves may differ from headline reserves

Potential constraints:

- pledged assets;
- illiquid components;
- forward commitments;
- derivative positions;
- sovereign/central-bank institutional arrangements.

Research should distinguish headline reserve stock from effective intervention liquidity when data permit.

## 17. Reserve adequacy is regime-dependent

A freely floating advanced economy with reserve-currency access has different needs from an EM defending a managed rate under external debt pressure.

Do not apply one “months of imports” threshold universally.

## 18. FX intervention — definition

FX intervention is official action intended to affect foreign-exchange conditions, commonly through transactions in FX markets or related instruments.

Intervention can be:

- spot;
- forward/swap;
- verbal/signaling;
- combined with liquidity/capital-flow measures.

Exact institutional definitions vary.

## 19. Buying vs selling reserves

Simplified:

```text
Central bank buys foreign currency / sells domestic currency
→ reserve accumulation
→ tends to resist domestic-currency appreciation
```

```text
Central bank sells foreign currency / buys domestic currency
→ reserve use
→ tends to resist domestic-currency depreciation
```

But market impact depends size, credibility, sterilization and macro regime.

## 20. Sterilized intervention

If central bank offsets domestic-liquidity effect of FX intervention, operation is sterilized.

Example intuition:

```text
sell domestic currency for FX
would expand domestic liquidity
→ separate domestic operation absorbs liquidity
```

Then intervention attempts to influence FX without equivalent monetary-base change.

## 21. Unsterilized intervention

If liquidity effect is not offset, intervention overlaps monetary-policy stance more directly.

Therefore empirical effect cannot be attributed only to “FX flow” if rates/liquidity also change.

## 22. Why intervention can work

Possible channels:

- portfolio-balance effect;
- signaling future policy;
- market-liquidity provision;
- coordination/expectation effect;
- stop-loss/positioning dynamics.

Effectiveness is state-dependent and empirically contested across settings.

## 23. Signaling channel

Intervention can reveal policy preference/information.

Market may infer:

```text
authority is uncomfortable with current speed/level
or
broader policy response may follow
```

But repeated ineffective intervention can weaken signal credibility.

## 24. Portfolio-balance channel

If domestic/foreign assets are imperfect substitutes, official changes in relative asset supply can affect required risk premium and exchange rates.

Magnitude depends market depth and substitutability.

## 25. Liquidity channel

During disorderly markets, authority may provide FX liquidity rather than defend an exact price target.

This distinction matters:

```text
restore market functioning
≠ promise specific exchange rate
```

## 26. Intervention against fundamentals

Defending a level while monetary/fiscal/external fundamentals point strongly opposite may consume reserves without durable effect.

Sustainability depends broader policy package.

## 27. One-way bet risk

If market believes a peg/band is unsustainable, intervention can invite speculative pressure because payoff becomes asymmetric around known boundary.

Need analyze reserve adequacy, policy consistency and capital mobility together.

## 28. Impossible trinity intuition

Under simplified macro framework, it is difficult to simultaneously maintain all three fully:

```text
fixed exchange rate
free capital mobility
independent monetary policy
```

Countries choose different combinations/degrees.

This helps explain why FX regime changes monetary-policy flexibility and intervention need.

## 29. Peg, band, managed float, free float

Regimes lie on spectrum.

Labels alone are insufficient; actual behavior can differ from official label.

Research should observe:

- realized volatility;
- intervention;
- policy statements;
- reserve changes;
- band rules;
- capital controls.

## 30. De facto vs de jure regime

**De jure** = officially announced framework.

**De facto** = behavior inferred from actual policy/market outcomes.

A “float” can be heavily managed; a “band” can move frequently.

## 31. Central-bank balance sheet interaction

Reserve accumulation changes asset side of central-bank balance sheet and may create domestic liquidity unless sterilized.

Persistent intervention can therefore create:

- sterilization cost;
- interest-rate interaction;
- balance-sheet valuation changes.

## 32. Intervention data can be noisy

Reserve change is not identical to intervention because reserves also change due to:

- asset valuation;
- interest income;
- government transactions;
- portfolio management.

Need direct intervention data when available or carefully decompose proxies.

## 33. Valuation effects in reserves

If reserves contain EUR/JPY assets but reporting currency is USD, exchange-rate changes move headline reserve value even without transaction.

A drop in reported reserves may partly be valuation, not selling.

## 34. Forward intervention can hide in spot reserve data

Authorities may use forwards/swaps; commitments can affect future reserve/funding exposure without immediate spot reserve change.

Research should inspect forward positions where published.

## 35. Capital controls and macroprudential measures

Authorities can influence FX pressure through rules on:

- borrowing;
- derivative positions;
- reserve requirements;
- remittances;
- foreign portfolio access.

These are not the same as spot intervention but can alter currency demand/supply and arbitrage channels.

## 36. FX valuation models

Common model families include:

```text
PPP / REER mean reversion
BEER-style macro fundamentals
FEER-style external-balance equilibrium
terms-of-trade models
rate/yield-differential models
portfolio / risk-premium models
```

Each answers different question and relies on assumptions.

## 37. BEER intuition

Behavioral Equilibrium Exchange Rate approaches relate real exchange rate to macro fundamentals empirically, such as:

- productivity;
- terms of trade;
- net foreign assets;
- rate differentials.

Estimated “misalignment” is model-dependent, not observable truth.

## 38. FEER intuition

Fundamental Equilibrium Exchange Rate approaches ask which real exchange rate is consistent with internal/external balance under normative/model assumptions.

Because desired current account/output gap assumptions matter, FEER is not a pure data fact.

## 39. Model uncertainty dominates precise fair-value numbers

Two reasonable valuation models can disagree materially.

Therefore better output is often:

```text
valuation range
+ model assumptions
+ historical percentile
+ catalysts
+ invalidation
```

not “fair value = 1.0837”.

## 40. Valuation z-score pitfalls

Historical z-score assumes distribution/stationarity meaningful.

Structural changes in:

- inflation regime;
- productivity;
- policy framework;
- capital mobility;

can invalidate old mean.

## 41. Cheap currencies can get cheaper

Valuation has weak short-horizon timing power in many settings.

A cheap currency under crisis can continue falling because:

- deleveraging;
- reserve loss;
- external debt;
- political/institutional risk;
- trend/momentum.

Risk management cannot be delegated to “value eventually works”.

## 42. Intervention event study

A research design should store:

```text
announcement / transaction timestamp
pre-event trend
size/type if known
spot + forward + rates response
subsequent policy actions
market regime
```

Concurrent macro news is a major confounder.

## 43. Reserve-loss stress map

Useful stress questions:

```text
How fast are reserves changing?
How much short-term FX debt matures?
What is current-account/funding gap?
Which liabilities are FX-denominated?
Are controls tightening?
Is monetary policy consistent with FX objective?
```

Avoid one-ratio crisis rules.

## 44. REER decomposition

If REER appreciates, ask whether driven by:

```text
nominal FX appreciation
or
domestic inflation > partner inflation
or
weight changes
```

Same REER move can imply different mechanism.

## 45. Corporate competitiveness

REER is macro aggregate. Firm competitiveness depends:

- invoice currency;
- imported inputs;
- pricing power;
- production location;
- hedging;
- competitor currency.

Do not map country REER one-to-one into a company's earnings.

## 46. Asset-investor FX valuation

Foreign investor cares not only currency valuation but total hedged/unhedged asset return:

```text
asset local return
+ FX
+ hedge carry/basis
+ tax/cost
```

A “cheap currency” does not guarantee attractive foreign-asset return.

## 49. Mental checklist

Before using reserves/REER/intervention in FX thesis, ask:

1. What regime is actually operating?
2. Is intervention spot, forward, liquidity or signaling?
3. Are reserve changes transactional or valuation-driven?
4. What denominator defines adequacy?
5. Are capital controls/arbitrage constraints material?
6. Is REER methodology comparable through time?
7. Which valuation model defines “cheap/expensive”?
8. What catalyst closes misvaluation?
9. Could funding/momentum overwhelm valuation?
10. What evidence would falsify the thesis?

## 50. Kết luận

FX valuation là **slow anchor**, không phải automatic entry trigger. Intervention là **policy instrument**, không phải guaranteed trend reversal. Reserves là **capacity indicator**, không phải infinite firepower.

Một robust currency thesis phải nối valuation với external balance, funding, policy regime, positioning và executable instrument — sau đó vẫn phải state uncertainty và invalidation.
