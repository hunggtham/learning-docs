# Case 04 — Global USD Funding Stress 2020: khi dollar shortage xuất hiện ngoài nước Mỹ

Tháng 3/2020 cho thấy một point rất quan trọng: USD không chỉ là domestic currency của Mỹ mà còn là **global funding currency**. Khi global institutions cần dollar liquidity cùng lúc, FX market có thể phản ánh funding stress chứ không chỉ relative macro view.

Case này nối Forex với money markets, cross-currency basis, central-bank swap lines và global financial plumbing.

## 1. Vì sao non-US institutions cần USD?

Banks, corporations và investors ngoài Mỹ có thể có USD liabilities vì:

```text
Trade invoicing
Commodity finance
USD bonds
Bank funding
Derivatives collateral
US asset holdings
Hedging demand
```

Do đó một institution ở Europe, Japan hoặc Korea có thể thiếu USD dù domestic currency của họ vẫn đầy đủ.

## 2. Offshore dollar system

Nhiều USD liabilities được tạo ngoài US banking system.

Mental model:

```text
Non-US bank / firm
borrows USD
→ lends/invests in USD assets
→ must refinance or hedge USD exposure
```

Khi normal funding markets hoạt động, rollover có vẻ routine.

Crisis bắt đầu khi everyone tries to secure USD at same time.

## 3. COVID shock as liquidity shock

Đầu 2020, pandemic uncertainty làm markets reprice mạnh.

Participants tăng demand for cash and safe liquid assets.

Corporates drew credit lines; funds faced redemptions; institutions needed collateral and margin.

Result:

```text
Demand for dollars rises sharply
while market-making capacity and risk appetite fall
```

## 4. Dollar appreciation can be funding-driven

Một common macro narrative:

```text
USD up because US economy stronger
```

không phù hợp với March 2020.

USD có thể strengthen vì:

```text
Global participants urgently need USD liquidity
→ buy/borrow dollars
→ dollar funding premium rises
```

FX move therefore can come from balance-sheet demand, not growth optimism.

## 5. Cross-currency basis as funding stress signal

Covered interest parity links spot, forward and interest-rate differentials.

In stress, cross-currency basis can widen because borrowing USD synthetically through FX swaps becomes expensive.

Conceptually:

```text
Need USD
→ borrow local currency
→ swap into USD
→ pay extra premium
```

If many institutions compete for USD, basis can move sharply.

## 6. FX swap is funding instrument, not only trading product

A large share of global FX turnover comes from swaps.

During funding stress, FX swaps are part of the plumbing that moves dollars across currencies and maturities.

Therefore:

```text
FX swap market dysfunction
can transmit into spot FX, bond markets and credit conditions
```

## 7. Why balance-sheet capacity matters

Dealers intermediate funding and hedges.

In crisis:

```text
Volatility rises
Balance-sheet usage becomes expensive
Risk limits tighten
Dealer intermediation capacity falls
```

Even when there are buyers and sellers economically, market-making balance sheet can become bottleneck.

## 8. Dash for cash

Investors may sell even high-quality assets not because fundamentals deteriorated, but because they need cash.

This can create:

```text
Sell liquid assets
→ raise USD cash
→ Treasury / bond market stress
→ more collateral/liquidity demand
```

Price movement is then driven partly by financing constraints.

## 9. Margin calls amplify dollar demand

Suppose global portfolio holds risky assets financed with leverage.

Market falls:

```text
Asset value down
→ margin call
→ need cash collateral, often USD
→ sell assets or borrow USD
→ further pressure
```

This is leverage feedback loop.

## 10. Central-bank swap lines

Federal Reserve has standing dollar liquidity swap lines with several major central banks.

Mechanism simplified:

```text
Foreign central bank provides its currency to Fed
Fed provides USD to foreign central bank
Foreign central bank lends USD domestically
At maturity, currencies are swapped back at same exchange rate
```

This allows foreign central bank to act as local distributor of dollar liquidity.

## 11. Why swap lines reduce FX funding pressure

Without swap line:

```text
Local bank needs USD
→ compete in private dollar funding / FX swap market
→ funding premium rises
```

With swap line:

```text
Central bank can supply USD against local collateral framework
→ private scramble for dollars can ease
```

Swap lines therefore target funding market, not exchange-rate level directly.

## 12. March 15, 2020 enhancement

Fed and standing swap-line central banks announced coordinated changes:

```text
Pricing lowered to OIS + 25 bp
84-day operations added
```

Goal: improve access to term USD liquidity.

This matters because crisis participants may fear not only today's funding but funding several weeks/months ahead.

## 13. March 19 expansion

Fed established temporary USD swap arrangements with nine additional central banks, including Bank of Korea.

For several central banks, line size reached up to USD 60 billion.

This is a direct example of global dollar architecture reaching Korea during stress.

## 14. Daily operations

Standing swap-line central banks increased frequency of 7-day operations from weekly to daily.

Frequency matters:

```text
If funding need can arise any day,
weekly auction may leave institutions waiting.
```

More frequent operations reduce timing mismatch.

## 15. Take-up and signaling

Swap-line usage increased strongly.

By late May 2020, outstanding central-bank swaps reached roughly hundreds of billions of dollars, with especially large usage by Bank of Japan and ECB.

But facility effectiveness comes from both:

```text
Actual dollars supplied
+ confidence that dollars are available if needed
```

Backstop credibility can reduce precautionary hoarding.

## 16. Korea connection

Bank of Korea received temporary swap line access during March 2020.

This mattered because Korea has:

```text
large trade sector
foreign portfolio flows
banks/corporates interacting with USD markets
```

During global dollar stress, USD/KRW can move not only from Korea-specific fundamentals but also from system-wide USD funding demand.

## 17. FIMA Repo Facility

Fed also created temporary FIMA repo facility allowing foreign official institutions to obtain dollars against US Treasury securities.

Why?

Without facility, reserve managers needing USD might sell Treasuries outright.

```text
Need USD
→ sell Treasuries
→ Treasury market pressure
```

With repo:

```text
Pledge Treasuries temporarily
→ obtain USD
→ avoid outright sale
```

This connects FX reserve management with Treasury market functioning.

## 18. Safe asset can become funding collateral

US Treasuries are safe credit assets, but during cash scramble even Treasury market can face liquidity stress.

Distinction:

```text
Credit safety
≠ market liquidity at every moment
```

Same distinction applies to FX pairs.

## 19. Why spot FX alone is incomplete

If EUR/USD falls during dollar funding stress, reading chart only as:

```text
EUR weak / USD strong
```

misses mechanism.

Need inspect:

```text
Cross-currency basis
FX swap points
Money-market spreads
Swap-line auctions
Dealer balance-sheet conditions
Treasury market liquidity
```

FX is connected to funding network.

## 20. Cross-currency basis and covered interest parity

In textbook no-arbitrage world:

```text
Spot
+ forward
+ interest rates
```

align through covered interest parity.

Persistent basis indicates real-world frictions:

```text
Balance-sheet cost
Counterparty limits
Regulation
Funding scarcity
Collateral constraints
```

Therefore basis is not just pricing anomaly; it can measure system constraint.

## 21. Corporate dollar debt

A non-US corporation with USD debt but local-currency revenue faces two stress channels:

```text
USD strengthens
→ local-currency debt burden rises

USD funding spread widens
→ refinancing cost rises
```

Currency and credit stress can happen together.

## 22. Hedging demand can worsen funding pressure

Suppose institution owns US assets funded in local currency and hedges FX.

Large market moves change hedge requirements.

```text
Portfolio revaluation
→ rebalance hedge
→ use FX forwards/swaps
→ additional USD funding demand
```

Thus hedging itself can become procyclical.

## 23. Dollar smile nuance

USD sometimes strengthens in both:

```text
Strong US growth / high US rates
and
Global crisis / funding stress
```

But mechanism differs.

This is why same price direction must not be treated as same regime.

## 24. Liquidity transformation

Banks/funds may finance long-duration or less-liquid USD assets with shorter-term liabilities.

When short funding becomes scarce:

```text
Need to shrink balance sheet
→ sell assets
→ widen spreads
→ further collateral pressure
```

Again, maturity mismatch is central.

## 25. Why swap line is not FX intervention

FX intervention typically aims to influence exchange-rate conditions by buying/selling currency in market.

Dollar swap line instead aims to provide **funding liquidity**.

It may affect FX indirectly, but instrument objective differs.

Do not confuse:

```text
FX intervention
with
central-bank liquidity swap
```

## 26. Moral hazard vs crisis stabilization

Backstops can create debate about incentives.

But during systemic funding stress, authority may prioritize preventing liquidity spiral.

Research should separate:

```text
Ex-ante incentive question
from
Ex-post crisis stabilization question
```

## 27. What data should be monitored?

```text
Cross-currency basis
FX swap pricing
LIBOR/OIS or successor funding spreads
Commercial paper / repo stress
Treasury bid-ask / depth
Central-bank swap-line usage
FIMA facility usage
Dollar index / major pairs
Bank CDS / funding spreads
Margin and collateral conditions
```

Not all data are available in real time to retail researcher.

## 28. USD/KRW-specific stress dashboard

```text
USD/KRW spot
Korea sovereign/CDS or bank funding proxies
Foreign equity/bond flows
BOK USD liquidity operations
Fed-BOK swap-line announcements
Korea FX reserves
Cross-currency basis / swap points if available
Semiconductor/export fundamentals
```

Purpose: separate Korea-specific weakness from global dollar squeeze.

## 29. Why ordinary volatility models fail

March 2020 was not just “volatility higher”.

Market relationships changed because:

```text
Funding constraints bind
Dealer balance sheet changes
Cross-asset correlations rise
Liquidity disappears
Policy backstops activate
```

Regime-aware risk model must include liquidity/funding state.

## 30. Strategy implication

A macro strategy long USD may profit during funding squeeze, but if thesis says “US growth outperforming”, attribution is wrong.

Journal should separate:

```text
Growth/rate signal P/L
vs
Funding-stress beta
```

Otherwise researcher may think model works for wrong reason.

## 31. Portfolio implication

Positions that look diversified can all depend on dollar liquidity.

Example:

```text
EM bonds
Asian equities
Commodity exposure
Cross-currency carry
```

may all suffer when USD funding tightens.

True diversification requires factor analysis.

## 32. Counterparty implication

Funding stress raises counterparty and settlement risk.

Even if directional FX view is correct:

```text
Broker/dealer liquidity
Margin requirement
Settlement capacity
```

may change rapidly.

## 33. Reverse stress test

Instead of asking:

> What if USD strengthens 5%?

Ask:

> What combination of USD appreciation, funding spread widening and margin calls forces portfolio liquidation?

This is more useful for leveraged portfolio.

## 34. Mechanism map

```text
COVID shock
→ global uncertainty + credit-line drawdowns + redemptions
→ demand for USD cash/collateral
→ offshore dollar funding costs rise
→ FX swap / basis stress
→ asset sales and stronger USD
→ dealer balance-sheet pressure
→ Fed enhances standing swap lines
+ adds temporary lines including BOK
+ creates FIMA repo facility
→ more USD liquidity available
→ funding stress eases
```

## 35. Risk checklist derived from the case

For any global portfolio, ask:

```text
Where are USD liabilities?
Which positions require USD margin?
How much debt matures within 3 months?
What hedges require rolling FX swaps?
What happens if basis widens sharply?
Which central-bank backstops exist?
Which counterparties can access them?
What assets would be sold to raise dollars?
```

## 36. Practice exercise

Hypothetical Korean institution:

```text
USD assets = 5bn
USD liabilities = 3bn short term
KRW funding swapped into USD = 1bn
Cash USD = 0.3bn
```

Stress:

```text
50% of short-term USD funding does not roll
Cross-currency funding cost +200 bp
USD/KRW +12%
Collateral call = 0.5bn USD
```

Calculate immediate USD liquidity gap.

Then assume BOK provides USD auction access through Fed swap line and institution can obtain 1bn USD. Recalculate gap.

Goal: see difference between **solvency, currency exposure and funding liquidity**.

## 37. What not to learn

Wrong lesson:

```text
"Fed swap lines always weaken USD."
```

No deterministic rule.

Better lesson:

```text
Swap lines address dollar funding scarcity;
FX impact depends on broader state and expectations.
```

Wrong lesson:

```text
"USD strength means US fundamentals are strong."
```

Funding stress can strengthen USD during weak global/US conditions.
