# Lenin — organization, party, state, revolution và imperialism

Chapter trước đã giải thích vì sao Lenin xuất hiện như một transformation bên trong Marxist tradition chứ không chỉ là người lặp lại Marx. Bây giờ cần đọc Lenin như một theorist của **organization under repression**, **revolutionary transition**, **state power** và **capitalism in an imperial world system**.

Điều quan trọng là giữ ba layer riêng:

```text
Lenin's text and argument
→ Bolshevik organizational/political practice
→ later Soviet / Marxist-Leninist codification
```

Ba layer có continuity nhưng không đồng nhất. Nếu collapse chúng, ta dễ dùng later doctrine để diễn giải ngược mọi sentence của Lenin hoặc dùng một historical outcome để thay thế textual analysis.

## Organization problem: từ grievance tới political capacity

Lenin viết trong Russian Empire, nơi political repression, censorship, police surveillance và fragmented local activity khiến coordination khác rất xa open parliamentary politics. Vì vậy organization trong Lenin không phải một side issue; nó là answer cho một causal problem: **làm thế nào localized discontent trở thành sustained political force?**

Một mechanistic reconstruction là:

```text
local grievance
→ communication network
→ shared interpretation
→ durable organization
→ strategic coordination
→ capacity to act across time and place
```

Nếu một movement chỉ tồn tại khi spontaneous anger cao, nó khó survive repression hoặc coordinate complex action. Lenin therefore emphasizes press, trained organizers, continuity và nationwide political work.

## Spontaneity và consciousness: một debate dễ bị caricature

*What Is To Be Done?* thường được summarized như claim rằng workers tự mình chỉ đạt “trade-union consciousness” và political consciousness phải được brought from outside. Đây là một passage historically influential, nhưng không nên biến nó thành proposition rằng workers không thể think politically.

Context của Lenin là polemic against a current he labels “Economism”: ông phản đối reducing worker struggle to wages/hours while leaving wider political struggle aside. His organizational conclusion is that revolutionary politics needs a broader program connecting workplace conflict with autocracy, law, repression và state power.

The conceptual issue is **scope of learning and coordination**. Local actors have rich local knowledge; centralized organization may see patterns across localities. But center can also misread local reality. A serious Leninist organization therefore faces a two-way information problem:

```text
local experience → center
center-wide synthesis → local strategy
```

If only the second arrow survives, organization can become command without learning.

## Vanguard party: function trước label

**Vanguard party (đảng tiên phong, 전위당)** is often used to summarize Leninist party theory. Rather than memorizing the label, break its intended functions apart:

- preserve organizational continuity;
- aggregate information across dispersed struggles;
- produce political analysis;
- train organizers;
- coordinate timing and strategy;
- maintain action under repression;
- connect immediate demands to a broader program.

Once decomposed this way, the institutional risk becomes visible: whoever controls analysis, appointments, communication and strategy may accumulate power over the movement itself.

So party solves one coordination problem while creating another:

```text
fragmentation problem ↓
organizational hierarchy solves coordination
        ↓
principal-agent / accountability problem ↑
```

This trade-off is not unique to communist parties; all large organizations face it. But it becomes especially important when party claims authority to represent a class and later exercises state power.

## Democratic centralism: concept phải được periodize

**Democratic centralism** is strongly associated with Leninist and later Marxist-Leninist party organization, but its institutional meaning varied historically. At a minimum, it combines internal discussion/decision processes with expectation of unified action after a decision.

The key analytical dimensions are not slogan but rules:

```text
who can deliberate?
who sets agenda?
how are leaders selected?
can minorities organize?
how are decisions appealed?
what information reaches lower levels?
what happens after disagreement?
```

A system can call itself democratic centralist while answering these questions very differently across periods. Therefore later Soviet practice should not be projected backward as a timeless definition of Lenin's entire organizational thought.

## Party, class và substitution problem

A party can claim to articulate long-run class interests even when many workers disagree with it. This creates the **substitution problem**: when does organization represent a constituency, and when does organization substitute its own judgment for that constituency?

The chain must be made explicit:

```text
class position
→ interests
→ consciousness
→ organization
→ party leadership
→ state action
```

Every arrow contains a representation problem. There is no automatic logical identity between “working class” and “party speaking in its name.” Institutional mechanisms—selection, recall, competition, deliberation, transparency, internal pluralism—determine how strong that representation claim is.

## State: neutral referee hay class structure?

In *The State and Revolution*, Lenin reads Marx and Engels as rejecting the idea that state in class society is simply a neutral body above conflict. State apparatus—administration, police, military, law—helps stabilize a social order structured by class relations.

But “state serves class rule” can mean several different claims:

1. officials consciously act for a ruling class;
2. institutions structurally depend on property/tax relations;
3. legal order protects a pattern of property rights;
4. policy systematically reproduces certain social relations even without conspiracy.

These mechanisms should not be collapsed. A serious empirical analysis asks which one is operating in a concrete case.

## Paris Commune và institutional reconstruction

Lenin uses Paris Commune as a reference point for a transformed political form: delegates with stronger accountability, recallability, reduced separation between officials and ordinary citizens, and attempts to break with a standing bureaucratic-military apparatus.

The important move is from **who rules** to **how rule is institutionally organized**. Replacing one group of officeholders while leaving all incentive, accountability and coercive structures unchanged may not produce the transformation Lenin thinks necessary.

This connects directly with modern institutional analysis: personnel change and rule change are different interventions.

## Dictatorship of the proletariat: semantic meaning và institutional content

**Dictatorship of the proletariat (chuyên chính vô sản, 프롤레타리아 독재)** in Marxist vocabulary refers to class rule in a transition away from capitalism, not simply personal autocracy in the everyday modern meaning of “dictatorship.” That semantic point is necessary but insufficient.

Institutional content still has to be specified:

```text
who has political rights?
who can organize opposition?
how are representatives selected and removed?
what limits coercive agencies?
what counts as resistance by former exploiting classes?
who decides that classification?
```

Without answers, “class rule” remains an abstract category.

## Coercion và withering away: transition contains an internal institutional tension

Lenin argues both that a revolutionary state must be able to suppress resistance from exploiting classes and that state as coercive class apparatus should eventually **wither away** when class antagonisms disappear.

The difficulty is a mechanism problem:

```text
transition requires concentrated coercive capacity
        ↓
how does concentrated capacity become self-limiting?
        ↓
conditions for withering away
```

Organizations normally develop interests in survival, budgets, authority and control. Therefore the move from stronger transitional state to weaker/no class state cannot be assumed. It needs institutional mechanisms and historical evidence.

This tension does not itself prove transition impossible; it identifies the burden a transition theory must carry.

## Revolution: structure does not replace agency

Leninist theory does not treat economic contradiction as automatic revolution. War, state crisis, legitimacy loss, peasant/worker alliances, elite splits, organization and timing matter.

A useful decomposition is:

```text
structural grievances
+
state vulnerability
+
organizational capacity
+
coalition formation
+
trigger / crisis
→ revolutionary possibility
```

Possibility is not inevitability. Similar economic grievances can produce reform, repression, electoral change, coup, revolution or no major change depending on institutions and organization.

## Alliance problem: industrial workers trong largely agrarian society

A major Russian difference from the imagined trajectory of advanced Western capitalism was the size of peasantry. Revolutionary strategy therefore had to address worker–peasant relations rather than assume a predominantly industrial proletarian society.

This is one reason Marxism changes as it travels. A theory centered on capitalist class relations in industrial Europe encounters agrarian property, peasant households, nationalities and imperial peripheries. Leninist politics therefore contains more explicit alliance strategy than a simple reading of Marx's mature economic texts would suggest.

## Imperialism: five features as a historical model

In *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism*, Lenin highlights five interrelated tendencies:

```text
concentration of production → monopoly
bank + industrial capital → finance capital
capital export gains importance
international monopolist associations develop
territorial division among major powers intensifies
```

The model tries to connect domestic accumulation with international hierarchy and geopolitical rivalry. It was written in the context of World War I and the colonial world of the early twentieth century.

## Monopoly does not mean competition disappears

A common misunderstanding is that Lenin thinks monopoly abolishes competition. His framework is better read as a coexistence/tension: concentration creates large firms/cartels and financial groups, but competition continues among large capitals and states.

That matters because rivalry may shift scale rather than vanish:

```text
many-firm competition
→ concentration
→ oligopolistic / monopolistic structures
→ rivalry among large capitals and geopolitical blocs
```

Whether this describes a particular modern industry is an empirical question, not something the word “imperialism” can decide in advance.

## Capital export và uneven development

Lenin gives special importance to **capital export**, distinguishing it from simple export of goods. Capital seeks opportunities across borders where returns, resources, labor conditions or strategic control may differ.

This connects to **uneven development**: capitalism does not develop all regions at equal rates or in identical forms. Different positions in world economy can generate different political coalitions and revolutionary possibilities.

Modern FDI, global value chains and cross-border finance create obvious conceptual echoes, but one must not infer that every investment abroad is evidence of Lenin's entire imperialism theory. Measurement requires ownership data, profit flows, political influence, coercion, trade structure and alternative explanations.

## Imperialism theory: what would count as evidence?

To evaluate Lenin's model, decompose it into claims:

```text
market concentration trend
finance–industry link
relative importance of capital export
cross-border corporate coordination
territorial/geopolitical competition
causal link between these and war/colonial expansion
```

The first five are partly measurable historical claims. The last is a much stronger causal claim. It requires comparative evidence and cannot be established by showing only that monopolies and wars coexisted.

## From Lenin to Marxism–Leninism

Lenin contributes a cluster of developments: durable revolutionary organization, transformation of state apparatus, transitional class rule, alliance strategy and an account of imperial capitalism. **Marxism–Leninism**, however, is a later codification that organizes Marx, Engels and Lenin into an official doctrinal architecture.

The handoff is therefore:

```text
Marx: capitalist social relations and historical critique
        ↓
Lenin: organization + state + transition + imperialism
        ↓
later systematization: Marxism–Leninism
```
