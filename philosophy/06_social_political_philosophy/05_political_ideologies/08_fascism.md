# Fascism — ultranationalism, authoritarianism và anti-pluralism

**Fascism (chủ nghĩa phát xít, 파시즘)** là một historically specific family of far-right authoritarian mass movements nổi lên mạnh ở châu Âu đầu thế kỷ XX. Chapter này dùng fascism như một analytical category gắn với historical movements và institutions, không dùng từ này như insult chung cho mọi politics mà người nói không thích.

Prerequisite là [Nationalism](07_nationalism.md), [Conservatism](02_conservatism.md), [Republicanism](06_republicanism.md) và [Democracy, rights and public reason](../01_democracy_rights_and_public_reason.md). Fascism có interaction với nationalism và conservative social forces trong history, nhưng không đồng nhất với conservatism hoặc nationalism nói chung.

## 1. Core pattern: nation above plural individuals

Historical fascist movements khác nhau, nhưng scholarship thường nhận ra một cluster: ultranationalism, authoritarian leadership, anti-liberalism/anti-pluralism, militarized mass politics, hierarchy và suppression of opposition.

United States Holocaust Memorial Museum mô tả fascism như ultranationalist, authoritarian political philosophy đặt nation lên trên individual, phản đối pluralism và democratic government. Britannica cũng nhấn mạnh extreme militaristic nationalism, contempt for electoral democracy/liberalism và belief in hierarchy/elite rule.

Không phải every authoritarian regime = fascist; category cần historical/ideological features cụ thể.

## 2. National decline → rebirth narrative

Một recurring mechanism là narrative:

```text
nation once great
→ decline / humiliation / internal decay
→ enemies or corrupt elites blamed
→ unity + purification demanded
→ leader/movement promises national rebirth
```

Narrative này converts social complexity thành moralized friend/enemy structure. Economic crisis, war defeat hoặc institutional distrust có thể create receptive conditions, nhưng chúng không mechanically cause fascism.

## 3. Anti-pluralism

Pluralist democracy assumes legitimate disagreement. Fascist politics tends to treat dissent not as alternate opinion but as betrayal/obstruction of authentic national will.

Once a movement claims one homogeneous national will, opposition parties, independent press, unions or minority organizations can be framed as illegitimate fragments. This is institutional bridge from ideology to one-party/leader-centered rule.

## 4. Leader principle và personification of national will

Fascist movements historically relied heavily on charismatic/authoritarian leadership. Leader is presented not simply as elected administrator but as embodiment/interpreter of nation.

Mechanism:

```text
plural institutions seen as weak/conflicted
→ leader claims direct unity with people/nation
→ intermediary checks delegitimized
→ personal authority expands
```

This directly conflicts with republican non-domination and liberal constitutionalism because contestation/checks become portrayed as obstacles rather than safeguards.

## 5. Mass mobilization, not merely passive dictatorship

Fascism is not only top-down repression. Historical fascist movements built rallies, youth organizations, party militias, propaganda and mass participation to produce active collective identity.

This matters because authoritarian control can be socially mobilized, not just imposed. Sociology of fascism therefore asks how grievance, status anxiety, organization, media and violence interact.

## 6. Violence as political method

Historical fascist movements often normalized political violence and paramilitary action. USHMM notes fascism's embrace of paramilitarism and acceptance/celebration of violence when framed as serving national community.

Violence does several jobs simultaneously: intimidates opponents, signals commitment, creates in-group solidarity and weakens normal legal boundaries. Once violence is legitimated as purification/defense of nation, category of “enemy” can expand.

## 7. Hierarchy, equality and belonging

Fascist ideology rejects egalitarian universalism in favor of hierarchy and differentiated belonging. Nazi ideology added explicitly racist and antisemitic biological hierarchy, culminating in persecution and genocide.

Important distinction: not every fascist movement had identical racial doctrine, and Nazism is not a synonym for every historical fascism. But exclusionary hierarchy and subordinating individual rights to national community are central features in major historical cases.

## 8. Economy: neither simple laissez-faire nor socialist equality

Fascist regimes often retained private property but subordinated economic organizations to state/national objectives, restricted independent labor organization and pursued corporatist or command-like coordination in strategic areas.

Therefore labels based solely on “state intervention” are misleading. A state can intervene heavily for very different institutional/normative purposes. Fascist economic organization must be analyzed through ownership, autonomy, labor rights, cartel/corporatist coordination, military priorities and political control.

## 9. Fascism và conservatism

Conservatism often values continuity, inherited institutions and skepticism of radical redesign. Fascism historically could ally with conservative elites, but it also pursued radical mass mobilization, new political myths and destruction/transformation of existing democratic institutions.

Thus:

```text
conservative caution/tradition ≠ fascist revolutionary ultranationalism
```

Overlap in particular historical coalitions does not erase conceptual difference.

## 10. Fascism và nationalism

Nationalism can be liberal, anti-colonial, conservative, socialist or other forms. Fascism is a more specific cluster where nationalism becomes ultranationalist and is joined with authoritarian anti-pluralism, hierarchy, mass mobilization and often militarism.

Therefore:

```text
fascism ⇒ strong ultranationalist element
nationalism ⇏ fascism
```

This boundary is necessary to avoid both overuse and under-recognition of the category.

## 11. Institutional erosion route

Historical fascist takeover can be analyzed as process rather than sudden label:

```text
legitimacy crisis
→ normalization of anti-pluralist rhetoric
→ intimidation/violence
→ emergency powers
→ weakening independent institutions
→ one-party/leader rule
→ repression and exclusion
```

Sequence differs by country, and each arrow requires historical evidence. The value of the model is to identify institutional mechanisms, not predict every case.

## 12. Evidence boundary

Calling a current movement “fascist” is a contested classification and should not be inferred from one policy, strong nationalism or authoritarian style alone. A serious classification needs explicit criteria, historical comparison and evidence across multiple dimensions.

This library therefore uses “fascism” primarily for established historical ideology/movements and clearly attributed scholarly classifications, not casual contemporary labeling.

## Handoff

Mental model:

```text
ultranationalism
→ decline/rebirth narrative
→ anti-pluralism
→ leader-centered authority
→ mass mobilization
→ political violence
→ hierarchy/exclusion
→ institutional capture
```

The final [Comparative synthesis](09_comparative_synthesis.md) puts fascism next to liberalism, conservatism, socialism, anarchism, libertarianism, republicanism and nationalism using the same institutional axes rather than a one-dimensional left–right score.
