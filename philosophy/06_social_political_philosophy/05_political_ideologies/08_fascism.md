# Fascism — ultranationalism, authoritarianism và anti-pluralism

> **Mạch đọc:** [README](./README.md) là bản đồ owner của **Fascism — ultranationalism, authoritarianism và anti-pluralism**. Route đi từ ultranationalism và anti-pluralism → decline/rebirth narrative → lãnh tụ, huy động và bạo lực → nhà nước độc tài, để phân biệt cơ chế phát xít với mọi dạng authoritarianism hoặc patriotism.

**Fascism (chủ nghĩa phát xít, 파시즘)** là một historically specific family of far-right authoritarian mass movements nổi lên mạnh ở châu Âu đầu thế kỷ XX. Chapter này dùng fascism như một analytical category gắn với historical movements và institutions, không dùng từ này như insult chung cho mọi politics mà người nói không thích.

Prerequisite là [Nationalism](07_nationalism.md), [Conservatism](02_conservatism.md), [Republicanism](06_republicanism.md) và [Democracy, rights and public reason](../01_democracy_rights_and_public_reason.md). Fascism có tương tác (interaction / 상호작용) với nationalism và conservative xã hội (social / 사회적) forces trong lịch sử (history / 이력), nhưng không đồng nhất với conservatism hoặc nationalism nói chung.

## 1. Cốt lõi (core / 핵심) mẫu (pattern / 패턴): nation above plural individuals

Historical fascist movements khác nhau, nhưng scholarship thường nhận ra một cluster: ultranationalism, authoritarian leadership, anti-liberalism/anti-pluralism, militarized mass politics, hierarchy và suppression of opposition.

United States Holocaust Memorial Museum mô tả fascism như ultranationalist, authoritarian political philosophy đặt nation lên trên individual, phản đối pluralism và democratic government. Britannica cũng nhấn mạnh extreme militaristic nationalism, contempt for electoral democracy/liberalism và belief in hierarchy/elite quy tắc (rule / 규칙).

Không phải every authoritarian regime = fascist; category cần historical/ideological features cụ thể.

## 2. National decline → rebirth narrative

Một recurring cơ chế (mechanism / 메커니즘) là narrative:

```text
nation once great
→ decline / humiliation / internal decay
→ enemies or corrupt elites blamed
→ unity + purification demanded
→ leader/movement promises national rebirth
```

Narrative này converts xã hội (social / 사회적) độ phức tạp (complexity / 복잡도) thành moralized friend/enemy cấu trúc (structure / 구조). Economic crisis, war defeat hoặc institutional distrust có thể create receptive conditions, nhưng chúng không mechanically cause fascism.

## 3. Anti-pluralism

Pluralist democracy assumes legitimate disagreement. Fascist politics tends to treat dissent not as alternate opinion but as betrayal/obstruction of authentic national will.

Once a movement claims one homogeneous national will, opposition parties, independent press, unions or minority organizations can be framed as illegitimate fragments. This is institutional cầu nối (bridge / 브리지) from ideology to one-party/leader-centered quy tắc (rule / 규칙).

## 4. Leader principle và personification of national will

Fascist movements historically relied heavily on charismatic/authoritarian leadership. Leader is presented not simply as elected administrator but as embodiment/trình thông dịch (interpreter / 인터프리터) of nation.

Cơ chế (mechanism / 메커니즘):

```text
plural institutions seen as weak/conflicted
→ leader claims direct unity with people/nation
→ intermediary checks delegitimized
→ personal authority expands
```

This directly conflicts with republican non-domination and liberal constitutionalism because contestation/checks become portrayed as obstacles rather than safeguards.

## 5. Mass mobilization, not merely passive dictatorship

Fascism is not only top-down repression. Historical fascist movements built rallies, youth organizations, party militias, propaganda and mass participation to produce active collective định danh (identity / 식별자).

This matters because authoritarian điều khiển (control / 제어) can be socially mobilized, not just imposed. Sociology of fascism therefore asks how grievance, status anxiety, organization, media and violence interact.

## 6. Violence as political phương thức (method / 메서드)

Historical fascist movements often normalized political violence and paramilitary hành động (action / 동작). USHMM notes fascism's embrace of paramilitarism and acceptance/celebration of violence when framed as serving national community.

Violence does several jobs simultaneously: intimidates opponents, signals commitment, creates in-group solidarity and weakens normal legal boundaries. Once violence is legitimated as purification/defense of nation, category of “enemy” can expand.

## 7. Hierarchy, equality and belonging

Fascist ideology rejects egalitarian universalism in favor of hierarchy and differentiated belonging. Nazi ideology added explicitly racist and antisemitic biological hierarchy, culminating in persecution and genocide.

Important distinction: not every fascist movement had identical racial doctrine, and Nazism is not a synonym for every historical fascism. But exclusionary hierarchy and subordinating individual rights to national community are central features in major historical cases.

## 8. Economy: neither simple laissez-faire nor socialist equality

Fascist regimes often retained private thuộc tính (property / 속성) but subordinated economic organizations to trạng thái (state / 상태)/national objectives, restricted independent labor organization and pursued corporatist or command-like coordination in strategic areas.

Therefore labels based solely on “trạng thái (state / 상태) intervention” are misleading. A trạng thái (state / 상태) can intervene heavily for very different institutional/normative purposes. Fascist economic organization must be analyzed through quyền sở hữu (ownership / 소유권), autonomy, labor rights, cartel/corporatist coordination, military priorities and political điều khiển (control / 제어).

## 9. Fascism và conservatism

Conservatism often values continuity, inherited institutions and skepticism of radical redesign. Fascism historically could ally with conservative elites, but it also pursued radical mass mobilization, new political myths and destruction/transformation of existing democratic institutions.

Thus:

```text
conservative caution/tradition ≠ fascist revolutionary ultranationalism
```

Overlap in particular historical coalitions does not erase conceptual difference.

## 10. Fascism và nationalism

Nationalism can be liberal, anti-colonial, conservative, socialist or other forms. Fascism is a more specific cluster where nationalism becomes ultranationalist and is joined with authoritarian anti-pluralism, hierarchy, mass mobilization and often militarism.

Therefore:

```text
fascism ⇒ strong ultranationalist element
nationalism ⇏ fascism
```

This ranh giới (boundary / 경계) is necessary to avoid both overuse and under-recognition of the category.

## 11. Institutional erosion tuyến (route / 경로)

Historical fascist takeover can be analyzed as tiến trình (process / 프로세스) rather than sudden label:

```text
legitimacy crisis
→ normalization of anti-pluralist rhetoric
→ intimidation/violence
→ emergency powers
→ weakening independent institutions
→ one-party/leader rule
→ repression and exclusion
```

Chuỗi (sequence / 시퀀스) differs by country, and each arrow requires historical bằng chứng (evidence / 증거). The giá trị (value / 값) of the mô hình (model / 모델) is to identify institutional mechanisms, not predict every trường hợp (case / 사례).

## 12. Bằng chứng (evidence / 증거) ranh giới (boundary / 경계)

Calling a hiện tại (current / 현재) movement “fascist” is a contested classification and should not be inferred from one chính sách (policy / 정책), strong nationalism or authoritarian style alone. A serious classification needs tường minh (explicit / 명시적) criteria, historical comparison and bằng chứng (evidence / 증거) across multiple dimensions.

This thư viện (library / 라이브러리) therefore uses “fascism” primarily for established historical ideology/movements and clearly attributed scholarly classifications, not casual contemporary labeling.

> **Chuyển mạch:** Đặt trong câu hỏi lớn của **Fascism — ultranationalism, authoritarianism và anti-pluralism**, **12. ## Handoff

Mô hình tư duy (mental model / 사고 모델):

```text
ultranationalism
→ decline/rebirth narrative
→ anti-pluralism
→ leader-centered authority
→ mass mobilization
→ political violence
→ hierarchy/exclusion
→ institutional capture
```

The final [Comparative synthesis](09_comparative_synthesis.md) puts fascism next to liberalism, conservatism, socialism, anarchism, libertarianism, republicanism and nationalism using the same institutional axes rather than a one-dimensional left–right score.
