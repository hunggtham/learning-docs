# Economic History Việt Nam: đất, thuế, thị trường và sự thay đổi của resource flow

## Vì sao cần một trục kinh tế xuyên thời gian?

Timeline 03–23 cho thấy từng regime, nhưng một câu hỏi lớn chỉ thấy rõ khi zoom out: **xã hội Việt Nam qua các thời đại tạo ra surplus ở đâu, ai có quyền claim surplus đó, và resource được chuyển từ household tới state/market bằng mechanism nào?**

Economic history không đồng nghĩa lịch sử GDP. Với thời cổ–trung đại ta thường không có national accounts; thay vào đó phải reconstruct từ settlement, land regime, tax, corvée, trade, coin, port, granary, price, household strategy và material evidence.

Mental model xuyên suốt:

```text
land / water / labor
        ↓
production
        ↓
surplus
   ┌────┴────┐
 tax/tribute market/exchange
   ↓             ↓
state capacity   specialization
```

Khi land right, transport hoặc tax system đổi, whole resource flow đổi theo.

## Từ subsistence tới surplus: Đông Sơn và early state

Trong Đông Sơn và Cổ Loa, wet-rice agriculture, metallurgy và craft specialization cho thấy economy vượt khỏi household subsistence đơn giản. Massive earthwork và bronze production ngụ ý polity có khả năng mobilize labor/material trên scale lớn.

Ta chưa có tax ledger, nên không nên invent tax rate. Nhưng **labor mobilization** bản thân là form of extraction: state/elite có thể claim time và food support của population để xây rampart, làm weapon hoặc tổ chức warfare.

Đây là dạng resource transfer trước mature bureaucracy.

## Imperial administration và extraction

Khi Bắc Bộ vào Han/Chinese imperial system, written administration làm extraction legible hơn. Household registration, administrative units, tribute/tax và official route tăng khả năng trung tâm quan sát population.

Key concept là **legibility**: state thu resource hiệu quả hơn khi biết ai ở đâu, land nào produce gì, household nào owe obligation.

Nhưng formal rule không đồng nghĩa full control. Local elite và village network vẫn mediation giữa administrative command và actual production.

## Nhà nước độc lập và fiscal base

Từ thế kỷ X, Đại Việt phải sustain court, army, irrigation và diplomacy bằng domestic resource. Land/agricultural tax trở thành fiscal backbone vì majority output nằm ở agriculture.

State capacity có ceiling do transport: rice bulky, road poor, river seasonal. Vì vậy tax collection often relies on local storage, in-kind payment và layered intermediaries.

Premodern state không thể tax every transaction realtime như modern digital state.

## Ruộng công, ruộng tư và quyền sử dụng

Lịch sử land không thể nén thành “đất của vua” hay “đất của dân”. Nhiều form cùng tồn tại: village communal land, royal/temple estate, private holding, military estate và land granted under different rights.

Điều quan trọng là **bundle of rights**:

- ai cultivate;
- ai transfer/inherit;
- ai collect rent;
- ai owe tax;
- ai can reclaim land.

Hai plot có cùng crop nhưng economic relation rất khác nếu one household owns/use-right securely while another owes rent/labor.

## Thủy lợi là economic infrastructure

Dike, canal và irrigation increase expected output nhưng require coordination. Red River Delta particularly makes water management central.

Infrastructure creates collective-action problem: upstream/downstream benefit khác nhau; maintenance cost hôm nay nhưng flood prevention benefit uncertain.

State/village institution xuất hiện không chỉ để tax mà còn để solve coordination problem.

## Thương mại thời Lý–Trần: market không phải foreign import

Market existed long before colonial capitalism. River ports, coastal trade, coin circulation, ceramics và merchant community cho thấy Đại Việt nằm trong regional exchange network.

Vân Đồn là good checkpoint: island/coastal geography connected domestic products with maritime trade.

Market depth vẫn limited by transport, information và monetization. Household có thể produce mainly for subsistence while still participating periodically in market.

## Hội An và early-modern commercial expansion

Thế kỷ XVI–XVII, Hội An becomes major port in network linking Japanese, Chinese, Southeast Asian và European traders. Nguyễn realm benefited from customs, imported weapon/material và commercial flow.

This reveals political economy mechanism:

```text
port trade
 → customs revenue
 → access to silver/weapons/goods
 → stronger regional state capacity
```

Trade and warfare therefore reinforce each other.

## Frontier economy: land expansion không phải empty-land story

Expansion toward central/southern regions often converted land-use regime, settlement pattern và tax jurisdiction. Chăm, Khmer, Việt, Hoa và local communities interacted through trade, conflict, migration and administration.

Economic history must ask **whose land-use pattern was displaced/reclassified**, not simply “new land was opened”.

Mekong Delta development depends on canal, water control, market town and migrant network; ecology imposes acid soil, flood and salinity constraints.

## Tây Sơn: fiscal-military crisis

Late eighteenth century crisis shows how tax pressure, trade disruption and military competition can destabilize regime. Rebellion is not reducible to “people poor therefore revolt”, but fiscal stress changes coalition incentives.

Army needs grain/money; court increases extraction; commerce disruption reduces taxable base; local groups shift alliance. Đây là **fiscal-military feedback loop**.

## Nguyễn: integration cost of a long territory

After 1802, state controls unusually long north–south territory. Road, station, canal, provincial administration and registers become economic infrastructure because information and tax must move.

Gia Long/Minh Mạng state therefore invests in administrative standardization as much as physical construction.

A road is not only transport; it is tax, troop, document and market network.

## Colonial capitalism: production for distant market

French colonial rule restructures land, tax, plantation, mining, transport and export. Rice from Cochinchina, rubber plantation and mining connect local labor to global capital and commodity price.

Railway/port may increase connectivity but design reflects colonial priorities as well as local mobility.

Critical distinction: infrastructure can raise aggregate capacity while distribution of benefit remains unequal.

## Wage labor and new class relations

Plantation, mine, factory, port and administration expand wage labor. Worker sells labor time rather than primarily relying on household land.

This alters risk: harvest failure matters less directly for a salary worker, but unemployment/wage level becomes new vulnerability.

Urban consumer market grows together with print, school and service jobs.

## War economy 1945–1975

Long wars redirect huge resource toward military logistics. Territory fragmentation creates multiple currency, tax, aid and trade regimes.

Foreign aid becomes key resource flow in both North and South, though forms and institutional effects differ.

Economic history of war therefore asks who finances imports, how food reaches army/city, how infrastructure is destroyed/repaired, and how households survive disruption.

## 1975–1986: planning and scarcity

Postwar central planning tries to administratively allocate scarce resource. Price, ration, state enterprise and cooperative become primary coordination tools.

When official allocation mismatches local scarcity, informal market expands. Economic behavior therefore becomes hybrid even before official market reform.

This period is covered in detail in chapter 21.

## Đổi Mới: change of coordination mechanism

From 1986 onward, household incentives, price signal, enterprise autonomy, FDI and trade reshape allocation.

The deepest change is not simply “private sector grows”; it is that more production decisions become decentralized.

```text
central allocation
     ↓
hybrid state + market coordination
     ↓
more price / firm / household decisions
```

State remains major regulator/investor/owner in many areas, so binary “plan vs market” is insufficient.

## Global value chain

In 21st century, economic resource flow increasingly crosses borders multiple times. Imported chip/material can be assembled in Vietnam then exported.

Tax/land remain important, but customs data, FDI, intellectual property, standards, logistics and electricity now shape value.

The economy moved from **surplus grain problem** to **productivity/technology/value-added problem** without the old land/water problem disappearing.

## Continuities worth remembering

Three long continuities recur:

**First**, geography matters through transport/water but technology changes its meaning. River once dominates bulk transport; today highway/port/container alter cost map.

**Second**, state capacity depends on information. From household register to digital database, extraction/service need legibility.

**Third**, household adapts across regime. Farmer, merchant, worker and migrant combine formal rule with local strategy.

## Reading places as economic evidence

Cổ Loa → labor mobilization. Thăng Long → administrative/market concentration. Vân Đồn → medieval maritime trade. Hội An → early-modern port economy. Gia Định/Mekong canals → frontier commercialization. Colonial railway/plantation → export capitalism. Industrial parks around Bắc Ninh/Bình Dương → GVC economy.

A site becomes useful when tied to a flow of **labor, goods, money or information**.

## Recap và connection

Economic history Việt Nam is a history of changing coordination: kin/village networks, tribute/tax, bureaucratic extraction, market ports, colonial commodity chains, planning, Đổi Mới and global value chains.
