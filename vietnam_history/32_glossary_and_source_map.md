# Glossary & Source Map — Lịch sử Việt Nam

## Cách dùng

File này không phải dictionary độc lập. Nó chuẩn hóa các concept lặp lại trong library và chỉ ra **source type nào phù hợp với loại câu hỏi nào**.

## Core analytical terms

### State capacity — năng lực nhà nước

Khả năng của state để collect information/resource, enforce rule, provide infrastructure, mobilize military và implement policy trên territory.

Không đồng nghĩa “state mạnh về quân sự”. Một state có army lớn nhưng tax/database/service yếu vẫn có capacity profile không đồng đều.

### Legitimacy — tính chính danh / sự chấp nhận quyền lực

Cơ sở khiến rule được xem là có quyền cai trị: dynasty, ritual, law, ideology, performance, revolution, election/institution tùy period/system.

Legitimacy là analytical term, không phải assistant phán xét regime nào “xứng đáng”.

### Extraction — huy động/thu resource

Tax, tribute, rent, corvée labor, requisition hoặc other mechanism chuyển resource từ household/economy tới ruler/state/elite.

### Corvée — lao dịch

Obligation cung cấp labor thay/ngoài monetary tax. Premodern road, wall, dike and military logistics often rely on labor obligation.

### Land tenure — chế độ nắm giữ/quyền sử dụng đất

Bundle of rights concerning cultivation, transfer, inheritance, rent and taxation. Avoid collapsing all arrangements into modern private ownership concept.

### Fiscal-military state/system — hệ thống tài chính–quân sự

Relationship where warfare increases need for revenue and administrative extraction; fiscal reform in turn expands military capacity.

### Political economy — kinh tế chính trị

Study of how power, institution and economic resource influence each other; not simply “politics + economy” as separate sections.

### Frontier — vùng biên động

Zone of interaction, migration, trade and competing authority; not necessarily fixed line border.

### State formation — hình thành nhà nước

Process through which authority, extraction, territory and coordination become institutionalized above local community scale.

### Bureaucracy — bộ máy quan liêu/hành chính

Rule through specialized offices, records and officials. Early state can exist without mature bureaucracy.

### Legibility — khả năng làm xã hội “đọc được” đối với state

Census, land register, map, ID and database turn complex society into categories usable for tax/service/control.

### Path dependence — phụ thuộc đường đi lịch sử

Past investment/rule changes cost of future choices. Dike network, capital location or legal system can persist because replacement is expensive.

### Public sphere — không gian công luận

Network where people debate issues through press, association, school, meeting and later digital media beyond household/court.

### Collective memory — ký ức tập thể

Shared ways a community remembers/commemorates the past. Important historical object but not identical with verified factual reconstruction.

### Historiography — sử học sử / lịch sử của cách viết lịch sử

Study of how historical accounts are produced, sources chosen and interpretations changed.

### Periodization — phân kỳ lịch sử

Dividing continuous history into named periods. Useful model but boundaries are analytical conventions, not natural walls.

### Teleology — mục đích luận

Reading earlier history as if it inevitably aimed at present outcome. Avoid projecting modern Vietnam’s borders/state/national identity unchanged into ancient past.

### Global value chain — chuỗi giá trị toàn cầu

Production system where design, input, assembly and sale occur across multiple countries.

### Transition economy — nền kinh tế chuyển đổi

Economy in which coordination/property/enterprise institutions shift from one regime toward another over time; old/new mechanisms coexist.

### Agglomeration economies — lợi ích kinh tế do tập trung

Productivity benefits from density of firms, workers, suppliers and knowledge in city/cluster.

### Externality — ngoại tác

Cost/benefit of action falling on others outside direct transaction, e.g. pollution or flood effects.

## Source hierarchy: không có một hierarchy duy nhất cho mọi câu hỏi

The right source depends on claim.

### Archaeology

Best for settlement, material production, burial, fortification and chronology where text absent. Limits: institutional title/intention often inferred.

Examples: Cổ Loa excavation; Đông Sơn artifacts; Óc Eo sites.

### Epigraphy / inscription

Strong for names, patronage, ritual and official statements close to time. Limits: commemorative rhetoric and survival bias.

Examples: stone stele, Cham inscriptions, temple inscription.

### Court chronicle

Strong for court event, diplomacy, official appointments, disaster records. Limits: elite/state perspective and moral framing.

Examples: *Đại Việt sử ký toàn thư*, Nguyễn-era chronicles.

### Administrative record

Strong for tax, census, law, land and bureaucratic process. Limits: official categories may not equal lived reality.

### Foreign diplomatic/imperial source

Useful independent chronology and external relation. Limits: external categories, political agenda and incomplete local knowledge.

Examples: Chinese dynastic histories, French/US diplomatic archives.

### Newspaper

Strong for modern public discourse, advertisement, urban event and language. Limits: censorship, audience and publisher bias.

### Memoir / diary / letter

Strong for personal experience and perception. Limits: selective memory and individual representativeness.

### Oral history

Strong for lived experience, migration, local war/social history. Limits: chronology drift and retrospective reinterpretation.

### Statistical dataset

Strong for quantified modern pattern when methodology known. Limits: category changes, missing informal activity and comparability.

### Heritage authority / UNESCO

Strong for site description, legal status and preservation history. Limits: inscription status does not validate every associated legend.

### Modern academic scholarship

Best for synthesis and debate when peer-reviewed/source-based. Need compare schools and publication date when field evolves.

## Recommended source owners by topic

### Ancient / archaeology

Bảo tàng Lịch sử Quốc gia; Viện Khảo cổ học; peer-reviewed archaeology; major university presses.

### Heritage/sites

Cục Di sản Văn hóa; UNESCO World Heritage/Intangible Heritage documentation; local conservation centres, checked against scholarship.

### Premodern textual history

Critical editions/translations of chronicles; academic histories by specialists; Chinese/Cham/Khmer source studies when relevant.

### Colonial period

French colonial archives, Vietnamese press, scholarship on Indochina; avoid relying only on colonial administrative narrative.

### Wars 1945–1975

Vietnamese records from multiple sides where available, French/U.S./international archives, oral histories and modern scholarship. Distinguish source position explicitly.

### Post-1986 economy

World Bank, IMF where appropriate, WTO, ASEAN, Vietnam statistical/legal sources and peer-reviewed development research.

### Contemporary trend

Use recent dated data and distinguish current statistics from historical trend. Do not extrapolate old population/income figures as present.

## Keyword conventions

Vietnamese term comes first when natural. Important scholarly keyword is noted in English. Korean term is added only when it helps comparison with Korean-history docs—for example **khoa cử (civil examination / 과거제)** or **Hán văn (Classical Chinese / 한문)**.

Do not force Korean translation into every line; connection must be educationally useful.

## Names and changing place names

Use historically appropriate name in context, then modern locator when helpful:

- Thăng Long → Đông Kinh → Hà Nội across different periods;
- Gia Định/Sài Gòn → Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh depending time;
- Phú Xuân/Huế depending political context.

Do not silently modernize ancient place if it changes historical meaning.

## Evidence labels used in this library

**Established:** supported by multiple strong sources.

**Inference:** conclusion from material/text evidence, not directly stated.

**Contested:** specialists disagree on interpretation/chronology.

**Traditional / legendary:** meaningful inherited narrative not verified literally.

**Memory/heritage claim:** evidence about how past is remembered rather than proof event occurred as narrated.

## Citation discipline for future updates

When extending files:

1. verify date/name with authoritative or scholarly source;
2. for controversial political/historical issue, use multiple perspectives and primary/academic evidence;
3. distinguish event date from publication/source date;
4. avoid long quotation—paraphrase and cite;
5. update internal links when a concept gains canonical owner.
