# 1945–1954: revolution, state-building, First Indochina War và Geneva

## Điểm tựa và câu hỏi trung tâm

[`18_colonial_society_nationalism_communism_and_world_war.md`](18_colonial_society_nationalism_communism_and_world_war.md) kết thúc với Democratic Republic of Vietnam (DRV) được tuyên bố ngày 2 September 1945. Nhưng proclamation không đồng nghĩa uncontested sovereignty.

Câu hỏi trung tâm của chapter này là: **làm thế nào conflict từ postwar power vacuum biến thành một cuộc chiến decolonization ngày càng internationalized, và vì sao 1954 kết thúc French war nhưng không kết thúc conflict về future political order của Vietnam?**

## 1945: independence claim trong một international occupation framework

Sau Japanese surrender, Allied arrangements phân công việc giải giáp Japanese forces: Chinese Nationalist forces vào northern Vietnam, British forces vào south. French forces gradually returned, particularly in southern Vietnam.

Điều này tạo multi-layer sovereignty problem. DRV có revolutionary legitimacy và local control ở nhiều nơi, nhưng không được các major powers immediate recognition như một fully secure sovereign state.

Vietnamese political parties khác cũng hoạt động, một số có support/network từ China hoặc non-communist nationalist traditions.

Vì vậy late 1945 politics không phải simple DRV vs France binary.

## State-building under scarcity

DRV phải đồng thời đối mặt famine aftermath, low fiscal capacity, weak administrative reach và foreign troops. Currency, food supply, tax và local security là existential problems.

A new state needs more than flag:

```text
legitimacy
 + officials
 + revenue
 + food
 + coercive capacity
 + diplomatic recognition
 = survival probability
```

Nếu một component thiếu, external or internal rival có opening.

## Negotiation và strategic compromise 1946

Trong 1946, DRV và France ký agreements/negotiated arrangements nhằm postpone or structure conflict, nhưng fundamental disagreement về sovereignty và French Union relationship remained.

Cả hai side faced constraints. DRV wanted time to consolidate and reduce simultaneous threats. France sought to restore influence while managing postwar weakness and colonial commitments elsewhere.

Negotiation failure should not be reduced to one meeting. Local clashes, incompatible political goals và mistrust accumulated.

## December 1946: full-scale war

Fighting escalated into full-scale war in **December 1946**, commonly marking start of First Indochina War at national scale.

French forces had superiority in conventional weaponry, urban bases and mobility in some corridors. DRV/Viet Minh strategy increasingly relied on rural bases, political mobilization, guerrilla warfare and gradual development of regular forces.

This is not simply “weak side uses guerrilla.” The strategic objective is time: avoid destruction, expand organization, raise cost of occupation and wait for international environment to change.

## City vs countryside is too simple

French control was strongest in many cities, ports and transport corridors; Viet Minh/DRV influence extensive in many rural/base areas. But map changed repeatedly and often resembled networks rather than clean blocks.

A road can be French-controlled by day but insecure at night. A village can pay tax to one side and provide intelligence to another under coercion or negotiation.

Control therefore should be modeled as multi-dimensional:

```text
military presence ≠ administrative control ≠ political loyalty ≠ tax access
```

## Mobilization: war penetrates household

Both sides required labor, intelligence, food and manpower. War therefore reached village through recruitment, taxation, requisition, security operations and political campaigns.

For DRV, building a revolutionary state and fighting war were inseparable. Cadre networks, local committees and mass organizations helped collect resources and transmit policy.

For French side, pacification and creation/support of non-communist Vietnamese institutions became increasingly important because permanent occupation by French troops alone was costly.

## State of Vietnam and Bảo Đại solution

France supported creation of **State of Vietnam** associated with Bảo Đại, seeking a non-communist Vietnamese state within evolving French Union arrangements.

This created competing claims to Vietnamese statehood: DRV claimed revolutionary/national independence legitimacy, while State of Vietnam pursued international recognition and gradual transfer of authority from France.

These claims cannot be understood only as puppet vs authentic binary. Institutions had varying degrees of autonomy, social support and foreign dependence; their capacity evolved over time.

## 1949 changes the war: Chinese Revolution và Cold War

Chinese Communist victory in **1949** transformed strategic environment. DRV gained access to a friendly border, training, supplies and diplomatic recognition from People's Republic of China and Soviet bloc.

In response to broader Cold War concerns, United States support to French war effort and non-communist Vietnamese state expanded.

Thus a colonial war became more deeply internationalized.

```text
French–Vietnamese decolonization conflict
          +
Chinese Revolution / US containment / Soviet bloc
          ↓
Indochina war as Cold War theatre
```

Neither layer replaces the other.

## Border Campaign 1950: logistics architecture changes

DRV victory in the 1950 Border Campaign helped open/secure routes to China and weakened isolated French positions near the border.

Strategically, the major outcome was logistical: external aid could flow more reliably; regular-force capability could increase.

This shows why border control matters beyond territory. A border corridor can change weapon supply, training and diplomatic connectivity.

## Land reform and wartime state transformation

During the later war, DRV intensified agrarian policies and eventually land reform. The goal included redistributing land, weakening landlord power, mobilizing peasants and transforming rural class structure.

Implementation involved coercion and serious abuses in a number of areas, later acknowledged and subject to correction campaigns.

For historical reasoning, land reform must be analyzed simultaneously as social revolution, wartime mobilization and state penetration into village society.

## French strategy and Điện Biên Phủ

By 1953–1954, French command sought ways to force a major battle and disrupt Viet Minh movement/supply. **Điện Biên Phủ**, a valley in northwest Vietnam, became a fortified base intended to draw/contain opponent and protect strategic routes.

The plan underestimated Viet Minh capacity to move artillery, build roads/trails, mobilize porter labor and sustain siege logistics across difficult terrain.

Battle therefore illustrates a recurring lesson of this library: technology and firepower matter only if logistics can keep them functioning.

```text
French air-supplied fortified base
        vs
Viet Minh dispersed logistics + artillery positioning + mass labor
```

The French garrison surrendered on **7 May 1954**.

## Điện Biên Phủ site: landscape explains more than heroic narrative

When visiting Điện Biên Phủ, look first at valley geometry, surrounding hills, airstrip location and distance to external supply routes.

Ask how artillery reached high ground, how trenches approached strongpoints, and how thousands of porters/bicycles/road crews sustained flow.

This turns “decisive battle” into a systems problem: logistics, engineering, intelligence, morale and diplomacy converge.

## Geneva 1954: ceasefire and regrouping, not a permanent border settlement

The Geneva agreements ended hostilities between French Union forces and People's Army of Vietnam. The military agreement established a **provisional military demarcation line** near the 17th parallel and regrouping zones north/south of it.

The line was explicitly a military arrangement, not intended in the agreement as a permanent political boundary.

Large population movements followed during the regrouping period, including many Catholics and other northerners moving south and Viet Minh personnel/supporters regrouping north, while some movement also occurred in other directions.

## Elections and the Geneva political settlement: distinguish documents and signatories

The conference's Final Declaration envisaged nationwide elections in **1956** as part of peaceful reunification. However, the State of Vietnam did not accept the settlement in the same way as the signatories to the military agreement, and the United States did not sign the Final Declaration.

This distinction matters. Saying simply “all sides signed an agreement to hold elections then one side broke it” erases legal/political complexity; saying “there was no election provision” is also inaccurate.

The better historical question is why the provisional military division hardened into two rival state-building projects amid Cold War escalation.

## 1954 ends one war but creates a new system state

French colonial military dominance was broken, but Vietnam was not politically reunified. Two zones now had different governments, armies, international supporters and institutional trajectories.

This is a classic path-dependence moment:

```text
ceasefire line
  ↓
separate administrations + armies + aid networks
  ↓
different state-building trajectories
  ↓
future reunification becomes harder, not easier
```

## Recap và bàn giao

1945–1954 was simultaneously decolonization, revolution, civil-political competition and increasingly a Cold War conflict. DRV survived from weak 1945 conditions by building administrative/military networks; France gradually shifted from direct reconquest toward a French Union/non-communist Vietnamese state strategy; international support on both sides expanded after 1949.

Điện Biên Phủ ended French capacity to continue the war on existing terms. Geneva stopped hostilities but left a provisional division that quickly acquired institutional depth.
