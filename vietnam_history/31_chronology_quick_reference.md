# Chronology Quick Reference — Lịch sử Việt Nam

File này là **coordinate map**, không thay thế các chapter causal. Với giai đoạn cổ, niên đại truyền thống và niên đại archaeological không phải lúc nào trùng nhau; các mốc uncertainty được ghi thận trọng.

## Tiền sử và early state

- Khoảng thiên niên kỷ II–I TCN: các cultural sequence Phùng Nguyên → Đồng Đậu → Gò Mun; agriculture/metallurgy ngày càng complex.
- Khoảng thế kỷ VII TCN–đầu Công nguyên: Đông Sơn phát triển ở Bắc Bộ và vùng liên quan.
- Khoảng thế kỷ III TCN: Cổ Loa trở thành major fortified political centre; tradition gắn với Âu Lạc/An Dương Vương.
- Khoảng 207 TCN: Triệu Đà hình thành Nam Việt (Nanyue) ở khu vực nam Trung Hoa và Bắc Việt hiện nay.
- 111 TCN: nhà Hán đánh bại Nam Việt, đưa Bắc Bộ vào imperial commandery system.

## Bắc thuộc và local autonomy attempts

- 40–43: khởi nghĩa Hai Bà Trưng.
- 248: khởi nghĩa Bà Triệu theo truyền thống/sử liệu.
- 544: Lý Bí xưng đế, hình thành Vạn Xuân; cuộc tự chủ kéo dài qua nhiều phase tới đầu thế kỷ VII.
- 679: nhà Đường tổ chức An Nam đô hộ phủ.
- 722: khởi nghĩa Mai Thúc Loan (chronology/details vary by sources).
- 791: Phùng Hưng associated with local uprising/control of Tống Bình.
- 905: Khúc Thừa Dụ gains local control amid Tang collapse.
- 930s: Dương Đình Nghệ và Ngô Quyền tiếp tục contest southern Chinese control.
- 938: Ngô Quyền thắng Nam Hán trên Bạch Đằng — major break from direct northern imperial rule.

## Thế kỷ X và early dynastic state

- 968: Đinh Bộ Lĩnh establishes Đại Cồ Việt and imperial monarchy at Hoa Lư.
- 980: Tiền Lê begins under Lê Hoàn.
- 981: conflict with Song; Đại Cồ Việt retains autonomy.
- 1009: Lý Công Uẩn becomes ruler.
- 1010: capital moved from Hoa Lư to Thăng Long.

## Lý–Trần

- 1054: state name Đại Việt used under Lý.
- 1075–1077: Đại Việt–Song war.
- 1225: Trần dynasty begins.
- 1258: first Mongol invasion.
- 1285: major Mongol–Yuan invasion defeated.
- 1287–1288: third major Yuan campaign; Bạch Đằng 1288.

## Hồ, Minh occupation, Lam Sơn

- 1400: Hồ Quý Ly establishes Hồ dynasty.
- 1407: Ming conquers Đại Ngu; direct Ming rule begins.
- 1418: Lê Lợi begins Lam Sơn uprising.
- 1427: Ming forces defeated/withdrawal settlement.
- 1428: Lê Lợi establishes restored Đại Việt under Lê dynasty.

## Lê sơ and fragmentation

- 1460–1497: reign of Lê Thánh Tông; major bureaucratic/legal expansion.
- 1471: Đại Việt campaign defeats Vijaya, transforming Đại Việt–Champa balance.
- 1527: Mạc Đăng Dung establishes Mạc dynasty.
- 1533 onward: Lê restoration movement forms rival court.
- 1592: Trịnh–Lê forces retake Thăng Long; Mạc power survives in northern pockets afterward.

## Trịnh–Nguyễn and southern frontier

- 1558: Nguyễn Hoàng sent to Thuận Hóa, starting durable Nguyễn regional base.
- 1627–1672: repeated Trịnh–Nguyễn wars.
- Sixteenth–seventeenth centuries: Hội An becomes major international port.
- 1698: Nguyễn administration commonly associates Nguyễn Hữu Cảnh mission with formalizing Gia Định administration.
- Eighteenth century: intensified settlement, trade and political competition in southern regions.

## Tây Sơn

- 1771: Tây Sơn uprising begins.
- 1777: Nguyễn ruling house in south largely overthrown; Nguyễn Ánh survives and continues contest.
- 1785: Tây Sơn defeats Siamese forces at Rạch Gầm–Xoài Mút.
- 1786: Tây Sơn moves north, destabilizing Trịnh order.
- 1788–1789: Qing intervention and Tây Sơn counteroffensive; victory associated with Quang Trung in spring 1789.
- 1802: Nguyễn Ánh defeats Tây Sơn, becomes emperor Gia Long and establishes Nguyễn dynasty over unified territory.

## Nguyễn nineteenth century

- 1802: Huế becomes capital of Nguyễn state.
- 1820–1841: Minh Mạng reign; centralization and administrative reform.
- 1831–1832: provincial reorganization replaces major regional governorship structure.
- 1858: French-Spanish attack at Đà Nẵng; French conquest process accelerates.
- 1862: Treaty of Saigon cedes three eastern Cochinchina provinces to France.
- 1867: French control extends over remaining Cochinchina provinces.
- 1883–1884: treaties establish French protectorate framework over Annam/Tonkin; colonial system consolidates over following years.

## Colonial era and revolution

- Early 1900s: reformist/nationalist movements expand; Đông Du associated with Phan Bội Châu.
- 1919: end of Nguyễn civil-service examinations.
- 1930: Indochinese Communist Party formed; VNQDĐ/Yên Bái uprising also marks revolutionary crisis.
- 1940: Japanese forces enter French Indochina while Vichy French administration remains.
- 9 March 1945: Japan removes French colonial administration in Indochina.
- 1944–1945: severe famine in northern/central Vietnam.
- August 1945: August Revolution amid Japanese surrender and imperial collapse.
- 2 September 1945: Hồ Chí Minh declares Democratic Republic of Vietnam in Hanoi.

## First Indochina War and division

- December 1946: full-scale Franco–Viet Minh war begins.
- 1949–1950: conflict internationalizes strongly amid Chinese Communist victory and Cold War alignment.
- 7 May 1954: French position at Điện Biên Phủ falls.
- July 1954: Geneva agreements establish provisional military regrouping around seventeenth parallel and political framework; not intended as permanent international border.

## 1954–1975

- 1955: Republic of Vietnam established in South; Democratic Republic of Vietnam governs North.
- Late 1950s–1960s: insurgency and interstate involvement intensify.
- 1965: major U.S. ground-force escalation.
- 1968: Tet Offensive; major military/political turning point.
- 1973: Paris Peace Accords; U.S. combat forces withdraw.
- 30 April 1975: Republic of Vietnam government collapses; war ends with northern/revolutionary forces controlling Saigon.

## Reunification, crisis and reform

- 1976: Socialist Republic of Vietnam formally established; national political-administrative reunification.
- 1978–1979: Cambodia conflict expands; 1979 Sino-Vietnamese border war.
- Early 1980s: economic experimentation increases amid shortages/inflation.
- 1986: Sixth Party Congress launches Đổi Mới as official reform direction.
- 1987: Foreign Investment Law enacted.
- Late 1980s: household-oriented agricultural reforms expand.

## Global reintegration

- 1994: United States lifts trade embargo.
- 28 July 1995: Vietnam becomes seventh member of ASEAN.
- 1995: Vietnam and United States normalize diplomatic relations and open embassies.
- 2000/2001: U.S.–Vietnam Bilateral Trade Agreement signed/enters into force.
- 11 January 2007: Vietnam becomes 150th member of WTO.

## Twenty-first century anchors

- 2000s–2010s: manufacturing exports, FDI and urban-industrial corridors expand rapidly.
- 2010s–2020s: deeper integration through multiple regional/free-trade frameworks; digital economy expands.
- Early 2020s: COVID-19 stress-tests public health, labor mobility and supply chains.
- 2020s: aging, climate risk, energy transition, productivity and higher-value manufacturing become increasingly central structural questions.
